# Prompts by area

AI Bootcamp: Zero to Hero. From the bootcamp.

Do one lesson at a time. If you don't finish a lesson, don't move on until it's done. If it takes longer, that's okay. The skills below match the bootcamp checklist.

Paste your house rules at the start of a chat. These prompts are samples. Use Sample names. Do not paste client ID, banking, or mortgage numbers. Drafts only. You review. You send.

The Deal Card kit and the Letter of Opinion kit are in this same folder. Attach the kit. Do not retype it.

## Getting started

### House rules

When to use: Teach It Your Writing Style. The approval line is rule one: it drafts, you approve. Use it at the start of a chat after that.

```
I'm a REALTOR® with [Your Brokerage]. I work in [your area].

Voice: warm, short, plain language. No exclamation marks. No em dashes.
Greeting and sign-off: [how you normally write].
Words I do not use: [your list].

Drafts only. I review every word and I send it myself. Never send, post, or text a client on your own.
Flag anything I need to verify. Do not invent prices, dates, school facts, or legal conclusions.
If something should be confirmed with the brokerage, say so.
This is not legal advice.
```

### About-me note

When to use: Pick Your AI and Tell It About Your Business, so your AI always has the basics.

```
About me. Use these basics on every draft. Do not invent a brokerage, a market, or contact details.

I am a REALTOR® with [brokerage].
Market: [your market].
Who I serve: [buyers, sellers, or the people you actually work with].
Sign-off: [your sign-off].
Contact: [the phone and email you want on drafts].
```

### Approval line

When to use: Teach It Your Writing Style, as rule one of the email writing SOP. Client-facing work always gets your review.

```
Approval line. You may organize notes and checklists on your own. Anything a client would see, an email, a text, a post, or a letter, waits for my review. I send it myself.
```

### Three things I hate

When to use: Brain Dump the Tasks You Hate.

```
I'm a real estate agent. Ask me a few questions about my week, then help me pick three tasks I hate doing that you could help me with during this bootcamp. For each, tell me how we'd start and how much time it could save.
```

A sample answer, not yours: paperwork follow-up, CMA prep, writing listing descriptions.

### Email reply

When to use: Teach It Your Writing Style, and any time a reply is waiting.

```
Draft a reply to the email below. Warm, short, plain language. No exclamation marks. Draft only. I will review it and send it myself. Do not invent facts.

[Paste the email. Remove private client identifiers first.]
```

### Text reply

When to use: when you need a short message you will send yourself.

```
Draft a short text I can send. One to three sentences. Plain language. Draft only. I will review it and send it myself.

Situation: [showing follow-up, offer update, or check-in].
What happened: [a few words].
```

### Appointment summary

When to use: Teach It How You Keep Notes.

```
Turn these notes into an appointment summary. Give me key points, open questions, and what I must verify. Do not add facts that are not in the notes.

[Paste the notes. Remove private client identifiers first.]

### Text templates

When to use: when you are not writing the same three texts from scratch.

```
Write three short texts I can reuse: a showing follow-up, an offer update, and a check-in. Plain language. Leave a blank where the name and the detail go. I will edit each one before I send it.
```

### Inbox cleanup

When to use: Teach It to Clean Up Your Inbox. Good spot to catch up. Skip it if you do not want your AI in your email.

```
Here are my 5 oldest unanswered emails. Private details are removed.

Draft a reply to each one. Short. Warm. Plain language. Number them to match. I will review every reply and send it myself. Do not invent facts.
```

### Time-blocked day plan

When to use: any morning after Pick Your AI and Tell It About Your Business. While you are parked. Never while driving.

```
I'm a REALTOR®. Here is my messy list for today. SAMPLE DATA ONLY.

3 showings, 2 callbacks, an offer to review, and a client gift to order.

Give me a time-blocked plan with the income tasks first. Flag anything I should confirm with my brokerage. Do not invent client names or appointment times.
```

### Closing-week checklist

When to use: when a sample file is heading to closing. Anything legal is confirm with the brokerage.

```
Give me a closing-week checklist for a sample freehold purchase. Mark anything legal as confirm with the brokerage. This is sample data, not a real file. Do not invent dates, names, or figures.
```

### Post-showing follow-up

When to use: the same day as a showing. Alex and Jordan Sample are fictional. 123 Maple Sample St is not a real listing.

```
I'm a REALTOR® in Midland, Ontario. Here are my messy notes from a showing today. SAMPLE DATA ONLY.

Buyers: Alex and Jordan Sample. Saw 123 Maple Sample St. Loved the kitchen and the walk to the waterfront trail. Worried the yard is small for their dog. Asked about school bus routes and what similar homes sold for. They're pre-approved, want to decide in 2 weeks.

Please give me:
1. A short, friendly follow-up email I can send tonight (under 120 words, no em dashes, no exclamation marks).
2. Three questions to ask them on our next call.
3. A to-do list for me before that call, marking anything I need to verify myself.
Do not invent sale prices or school facts. Say "verify" where I need to check.
```

### Buyer consultation prep

When to use: before a first meeting. Search stays in MLS.

```
Build me a one-page prep sheet for a first buyer consultation with a sample couple relocating to Midland. SAMPLE DATA ONLY.

Include questions I should ask, a showing-day checklist, and what I must verify myself. Do not invent neighbourhood facts, school facts, or prices.
```

### Summarize a long note

When to use: for a long email or call notes. Check it against the original.

```
Summarize the notes below. SAMPLE DATA ONLY. Give me key points, risks, and questions to ask. Tell me what to verify against the original. Do not add facts that are not in the notes.

[Paste the notes here. Remove private client identifiers first.]
```

### Plain-language explainer

When to use: when a buyer needs a short explanation. This is general education, not legal advice.

```
Explain, in plain language a buyer can follow, what a home inspection condition means. I am a REALTOR® in Ontario. Keep it short. Flag anything I should confirm. This is general education, not legal advice. Do not invent rules.
```

## Letter of Opinion

When to use: Make the Letter of Opinion Yours, then Practise, then Time a Real Letter. That second one is a good spot to catch up. Attach Letter-of-Opinion-Kit.md. The opinion of value is yours. You check every name and every figure, and you send it.

```
I'm a REALTOR®. Draft a Letter of Opinion and the client email. Use my template, my signature photo, and the Letter of Opinion kit. SAMPLE DATA ONLY. Do not invent a comp, a price, or a postal code.

People on title: Alex Sample and Jordan Sample.
Person who asked: Alex Sample.
Address: 123 Sample Street, Midland, ON. No postal code. Do not invent one.
My opinion: market value between $480,000 and $510,000. Maximum recommended list price $525,000.
Purpose: for their accountant.

Comps I am giving you. All fictional:
- 10 Sample Crescent, Midland. Sold $495,000.
- 18 Sample Crescent, Midland. Active, listed $529,000.
- 7 Sample Court, Midland. Sold $502,000.

One page. Draft only. I will check every figure and send it myself.
```

## Voice memo and appointment recap

When to use: Teach It Your Writing Style, when you talk instead of type, and Teach It How You Keep Notes, when a note has to land in your CRM. Let people know you are recording.

Give me:
1. A 3-line CRM note
2. A follow-up task list with dates
3. A short follow-up email recapping what they told me and the next step

No em dashes. Flag anything I need to verify. Do not invent details.
```

## Best practices

### End of each chat

When to use: Set Up Your Decision Log, and at the end of every lesson after that. Save the list to your decision log. An SOP is a step-by-step. Teach It Your Writing Style is where you save the email writing SOP.

```
Before we finish, list any new SOPs and decisions from this chat in short bullet points so I can save them to my rules file.
```

### Read my rules back

When to use: Set Up Your Decision Log and Final Review, and at the start of a fresh chat, or when a thread gets muddled.

```
Read my house rules back to me in short bullets before we start. If anything is missing, ask. Do not invent a rule I did not give you.
```
