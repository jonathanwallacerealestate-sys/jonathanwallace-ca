Getting Ready to Sell in Spring: Your Fall-to-Spring Checklist for Georgian Bay Homes

By Jonathan Wallace, REALTOR® with Faris Team Real Estate Brokerage

Midland · Penetanguishene · Tiny · Tay · Wasaga Beach · ~12 min read

Last updated October 12, 2026

TL;DR

The short version

  • October to November: fall exterior photos, pre-listing inspection, septic pump-out (parging only above about 10°C), deck railings, attic check, furnace and A/C service.
  • December to January: work through the inspection list, paint over old water stains, declutter, start your house book.
  • February to March: paperwork, three quotes for anything you can't fix, pricing, and booking trades.
  • Last 2 to 4 weeks: spring walk-around, deep clean, interior photos, house book on the counter.
If you're planning to sell in the spring, start now. The best spring listings I've worked on around Georgian Bay were put together the fall before. The outside work has to be done before the ground freezes, the inside work fills the winter, and by March you're pricing and booking trades, not scrambling.

Most people think about selling in the spring and start getting ready in April. By then the lot is wet, the snowbanks are grey, and every contractor in North Simcoe is booked. Selling isn't really about a fresh coat of paint. It's about taking away every reason a buyer has to hesitate or knock money off your price. That takes time, and in our part of Ontario, the weather decides what you can do and when.

Here's how I'd lay it out. Each topic below has its own page with the details: the month-by-month timeline, the room-by-room list, what's worth fixing (and what isn't), and the paperwork that makes rural and waterfront homes easier to sell.

Frequently asked questions

When should I start getting my house ready to sell in the spring?

Start in October. Around Georgian Bay, the outside jobs (fall photos, septic, deck repairs, anything involving cement) have to happen before the freeze. Winter is for the inside work and the paperwork, and February and March are for pricing and booking your photographer and trades. If you wait until April, you're doing everything at once, in the mud.

Should I take my listing photos in the fall if I'm selling in the spring?

Yes, at least the exterior shots. In April, exteriors look drab, the lot is wet, nothing is growing and the trees are bare. Fall photos of your yard, gardens and water in colour can sit alongside fresh interior photos taken right before you list.

Is a pre-listing home inspection worth it?

In my opinion, yes, especially if you're listing in the spring. An inspection now gives you the whole winter to fix, price for or disclose what it finds, instead of hearing about it for the first time from the buyer's inspector, when it gets used to negotiate your price down.

Do I need to pump and inspect my septic before I sell?

I'd do it before you list. Buyers will ask for it, and most offers on septic properties call for it anyway. Do it in the fall while the ground is open, and keep the hauler's written report for your house book. If the tank needs parging inside, that has to happen while it's still warm enough for the cement to cure properly.

Should I paint over an old water stain if the leak was fixed years ago?

Yes. Fix the cause first, then prime and paint so the stain is gone, and keep the repair invoice in your house book. Painting doesn't replace disclosing a known issue. What it does is take away the buyer's "is this still leaking?" question. When they ask, you hand them the invoice.

What if something needs replacing and I can't afford to do it before I sell?

Get three written quotes before you list. Buyers almost always guess high, and that guess becomes their negotiating number. With three quotes in hand, you can answer with real numbers instead of arguing about a guess.

What is a house book, and do buyers actually look at it?

It's a binder (or a shared folder) with your service records, receipts, invoices, warranties, manuals and a list of what's been updated and when. Buyers do read it. Leave it on the kitchen counter for showings. Every question it answers is one less objection later.

What extra paperwork do I need to sell a cottage or rural home on Georgian Bay?

Usually a septic pump-out report and permit history, a recent well water test, a WETT inspection for any wood stove, information on any oil tank, your survey, and any shoreline or dock permits. If you have private-road access, you'll need your road or snowplowing agreement too. Start gathering these in the fall. Some take weeks.
