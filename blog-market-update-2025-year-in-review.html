2025 in Review: Sellers Listed as Many Homes as 2024. Buyers Bought 1,555 Fewer.

Year in review·2025 year in review·~7 min read
Thinking of selling? Book a home evaluation

By Jonathan Wallace, REALTOR® · 2025 year in review

Simcoe County recorded 7,703 sales in 2025 at an average sale price of $790,637. That is 1,555 fewer sales than 2024, a drop of 16.8 percent. Sellers did not hold back. 26,081 new listings came on, slightly more than the 25,968 of 2024. That gap between steady supply and shrinking demand is what turned 2025 into a buyer's market from February on.

Key takeaways

  • 7,703 sales, down 16.8 percent from 9,258 in 2024, and the fewest of the past five years.
  • New listings were 26,081, up 0.4 percent, so supply held while sales fell.
  • The average sale price was $790,637, down 3.2 percent. Prices fell far less than sales.
  • Months of inventory was above six, the buyer's market line, in every month from February to December.
  • Midland and Penetanguishene sales fell 34.3 percent and Tay and Tiny 31.0 percent, the steepest drops on the Bay. Orillia was nearly flat.

The year buyers stepped back and sellers did not

In a typical slowdown, sellers pull back alongside buyers. In 2025 only one side did. New listings across the county were level with 2024. Sales fell by roughly one in six. The backlog grew: active listings finished December at 3,249, up from 2,790 a year earlier.

January was the last month under six months of inventory, at 5.74. By February it was 6.37, and it never came back below six for the rest of the year. The peak was November, at 8.02.

Prices bent, they did not break

For a year with 16.8 percent fewer sales, the average price held up well, down 3.2 percent to $790,637. Homes sold at 97.1 percent of asking on average, against 97.5 percent in 2024, and took 42 days compared with 40. Sellers gave up a little on price and a little on time. The larger cost of 2025 was certainty: fewer homes sold at all.

The board reports Midland and Penetanguishene as one market, and Tay and Tiny as one market. For Midland and Penetanguishene, the 2024 year-end report does not include an average price, days on market or list to sale ratio, so no 2024 comparison is shown for those.

Midland and Penetanguishene

The steepest drop on the Bay. Sales fell to 391 from 595. Sellers did pull back here, with new listings down 15.7 percent to 1,206, but buyers pulled back twice as hard. Homes that sold went at 97.3 percent of asking, second only to Orillia among the six markets, in 44 days.

Tay Township and Tiny Township

Sales fell 31.0 percent to 416. Unusually, the average price rose 2.2 percent to $744,470. Homes took longer, 54 days against 48, and sold at 96.2 percent of asking. Fewer homes sold, but those that did held their value on average.

Wasaga Beach

Sales fell 28.1 percent to 546, and the average price slipped 1.3 percent to $658,363. Days on market actually improved, to 50 from 52. New listings fell 12.9 percent, so supply eased more here than in the county overall. Wasaga finished December at 14.26 months of inventory, a sign of how quiet the off-season gets.

Severn

A milder year. Sales fell 15.8 percent to 224 and the average price fell 5.7 percent to $865,467. Homes sold faster than in 2024, in 45 days against 49.

Collingwood and The Blue Mountains

Sales fell 26.9 percent to 812, and new listings fell almost as much, down 25.1 percent. The average price was $907,892, down 6.3 percent. This market spans Simcoe County and Grey County, so it is not counted inside the county totals.

Orillia

The steadiest market in the region. 506 sales against 511, homes sold in 37 days, and at 97.5 percent of asking. Even here the average price fell 5.1 percent, to $630,780, while new listings rose 12.6 percent.

What 2025 set up for 2026

2025 ended with 7.85 months of inventory in December. That is where 2026 started: plenty of choice for buyers, and sellers competing with a backlog. The first months of 2026 ran even slower before the spring caught up, as the January to May 2026 review shows.

Common questions about the 2025 market

How many homes sold in Simcoe County in 2025?

7,703, down 16.8 percent from 9,258 in 2024, at an average sale price of $790,637.

Was 2025 a buyer's market in Simcoe County?

Yes. Months of inventory was above six, the line for a buyer's market, in every month from February to December 2025. It peaked at 8.02 in November.

Did home prices fall in Simcoe County in 2025?

A little. The average sale price fell 3.2 percent, from $816,883 in 2024 to $790,637. Sales fell much more, by 16.8 percent.

Which Georgian Bay market slowed the most in 2025?

Midland and Penetanguishene, where sales fell 34.3 percent to 391. Tay and Tiny was close behind, down 31.0 percent to 416.

How long did homes take to sell in Tay Township and Tiny Township in 2025?

54 days on average, up from 48 in 2024. When a listing is cancelled and put back on the market, the days on market count starts over, so some homes were for sale longer than the number shows.
