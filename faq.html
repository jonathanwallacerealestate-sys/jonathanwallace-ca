What are the best communities on southern Georgian Bay?

There is no single best, only the best fit for how you live. Midland is the hub, with the harbour, hospitals, schools and services. Penetanguishene is the historic waterfront town next door, often the more affordable entry point. Tay Township, including Victoria Harbour and Port McNicoll, offers small-community living along the Trans-Canada Trail and the water. Tiny Township is beaches and cottage country, with some of the clearest water on the Bay. Wasaga Beach is the world's longest freshwater beach with a fast-growing year-round community. Each has its own guide on the Communities page.

What should I know before buying waterfront property on Georgian Bay?

Waterfront on Georgian Bay is a different purchase from a home in town, and the differences are where deals go sideways. Shoreline type and exposure, water levels, septic and well condition, road access in winter, and shoreline regulations all affect value and daily life. Walk the property, ask what the water does in November as well as July, and work with someone who has toured and sold along this shoreline. How do 2026 property tax rates compare in Midland, Penetanguishene, Tiny, Tay, and Wasaga Beach?

2026 residential property tax rates, including the municipal, County of Simcoe, and education portions, are highest in Midland at 1.770017%, then Penetanguishene at 1.68599%, Tay Township at 1.265389%, Wasaga Beach at 1.223162%, and Tiny Township at 0.918813%. On $100,000 of assessed value, that is about $1,770 in Midland and $919 in Tiny. Your bill is the rate multiplied by your MPAC assessment, so confirm the roll for the specific property.

Sources: Town of Midland, By-law 2026-29, Schedule A; Town of Penetanguishene, 2026 Final Tax Bill insert; Township of Tiny, By-law 26-025; Township of Tiny, 2026 tax-rate spreadsheet; Township of Tay, By-law 2026-19, Schedule A; Town of Wasaga Beach, By-law 2026-34, Schedule A.

Can I operate a short-term rental in Tiny, Penetanguishene, Midland, Tay, or Wasaga Beach?

Short-term rental rules differ by town, so confirm the address before you buy for rental income. Tiny requires a licence for stays of 28 days or less and caps licences at 300. Penetanguishene requires a licence for stays of 28 consecutive days or less. Wasaga Beach requires a licence for rentals of 30 days or less, plus Municipal Accommodation Tax. Midland is still writing its by-law, expected in Q2 2027. In Tay, confirm permitted use with planning staff first.

Sources: Township of Tiny, Short-Term Rentals; Town of Penetanguishene, By-law 2022-58; Town of Wasaga Beach, Short-Term Accommodations; Town of Wasaga Beach, By-law 2026-08; Town of Midland, by-law under development.

How does Tiny Township's septic re-inspection program work?

Tiny Township inspects private sewage systems on a cycle that covers the township in about six years. When your area is selected, you get a letter. Tatham Engineering, working for the Township, does a visual inspection in the non-winter months. You must also have a licensed hauler pump the tank and file a written report with Tatham. If a deficiency is found, you must correct it. This is separate from the septic inspection in your offer.

Sources: Township of Tiny, Re-Inspection Program; Township of Tiny, Private Sewage Systems. For offer wording, see what a septic clause in an Ontario offer should protect.

How do I test well water in Midland, Tiny, or Tay?

Bacterial testing of private well water for E. coli and total coliforms is free through Public Health Ontario. Pick up a sterile bottle at the Simcoe Muskoka District Health Unit office in Midland, 925 Hugel Avenue, Unit A, and drop the sample off Monday to Thursday before 4:30 p.m. Collect it just before drop-off and keep it cool. Do not leave samples at health unit offices on Fridays. Chemical testing requires a private licensed laboratory.

Sources: Simcoe Muskoka District Health Unit, Free Drinking Water Testing (updated May 6, 2026); Public Health Ontario, Well Water Testing.

Which roads in Tiny are municipally maintained in winter, and which close?

Tiny Township maintains about 470 km of public roads and generally gives priority to major class 3 routes. Some public sections have no winter maintenance and are closed for the season. As of November 2, 2026, the hills on Concessions 8, 15, and 17 West, Hall Road, and most of Stamp Sideroad are closed until further notice. Before buying a winter home, confirm the road is assumed and municipally maintained, or that a private plough is arranged.

Sources: Township of Tiny, Winter Maintenance; Township of Tiny, Annual Winter Road Closures notice, November 2, 2026; Township of Tiny, Roads in Tiny.

What are Georgian Bay water levels right now?

Georgian Bay is part of Lake Michigan-Huron. For the week ending November 23, 2026, Fisheries and Oceans Canada reported a Lake Huron weekly mean of 176.39 metres IGLD 1985 at Goderich, 0.39 metres above chart datum and 0.06 metres lower than the previous week. Levels change through the year, so use the current weekly bulletin when you walk a shoreline. Inland lakes such as Farlain Lake are a separate story.

Sources: Fisheries and Oceans Canada, Weekly Water Level Bulletin. For the inland lake, see Farlain Lake high water.

Are real estate commissions fixed in Ontario?

No. In Ontario, what you pay a brokerage is negotiated between you and the brokerage. The Real Estate Council of Ontario does not set or approve commission rates, and neither does the government or a real estate board. The amount can be a fixed dollar figure, a percentage of the sale price, or both, and it must be written into your representation agreement. A seller's agreement should also state any amount offered toward the buyer's brokerage fees.

Sources: RECO, Signing a contract with a real estate brokerage.

Which schools would a home in Midland, Penetanguishene, Tiny, Tay, or Wasaga Beach go to?

School assignment depends on the exact address and the board. Public schools are assigned by the Simcoe County District School Board, and Catholic schools by the Simcoe Muskoka Catholic District School Board. French-language boards also serve the area, including École élémentaire catholique Sainte-Croix in Lafontaine. Tiny Township has no public high school; this site's Tiny guide says students are zoned to Georgian Bay District Secondary School in Midland. Confirm the catchment on the board locators.

Sources: Simcoe County District School Board; Simcoe Muskoka Catholic District School Board.

How good is internet in Tiny, Tay, Midland, Penetanguishene, and Wasaga Beach?

Broadband quality varies sharply between in-town Midland, Penetanguishene, and Wasaga Beach, and rural concessions in Tiny and Tay. There is no single provider or speed for the whole shore. Check the specific address on Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada's National Broadband Internet Service Availability Map, then confirm with the providers shown for that location, before you assume you can work remotely or stream reliably.

Sources: ISED, National Broadband Internet Service Availability Map.

What is the 66-foot shore road allowance in Tiny Township?

It is a strip, typically 66 feet, reserved in the original Crown surveys between many waterfront lot lines and Georgian Bay. On one lot a previous owner may have purchased that strip. On the next lot it may still be public, even if it has been mowed like a yard. Those two cottages do not sell as the same product. Confirm ownership with the deed, a survey, and the Township of Tiny before you price the sand as private.

Can I build or alter the shoreline on a Tiny Township waterfront lot right now?

According to the Township of Tiny's official interim-control page, Interim Control By-law 23-044 remains in effect until the Ontario Land Tribunal appeal of Zoning By-law 25-039 is complete. New structures, fences, boathouses, retaining walls, patios, decks, landscaping, and site alteration near the 178-metre Georgian Bay contour are prohibited while that is the case. Email interimcontrol@tiny.ca before any shoreline work, and do not assume the 2025 shoreline permit system is in force.

Sources: Township of Tiny, Interim Control By-law (checked November 27, 2026). Longer context: who owns the beach in Tiny Township.

How far is the commute from Midland or Penetanguishene to Barrie or Toronto?

Midland and Penetanguishene sit on Highways 12 and 93, with Highway 400 and GO Transit rail connections beginning in Barrie. A daily Toronto commute is long. Many buyers who work in the GTA live here on a hybrid schedule and drive south a few days a week. Wasaga Beach is generally the closest of these five municipalities to the Highway 400 corridor. Time the drive on a weekday before you commit.

Do I pay Ontario land transfer tax when I buy in Midland, Penetanguishene, or Tiny?

Yes. Ontario land transfer tax applies here at 0.5% on the first $55,000, 1% up to $250,000, 1.5% up to $400,000, and 2% above $400,000, rising to 2.5% above $2 million where the land contains one or two single-family residences. Qualifying first-time buyers may receive a refund of up to $4,000, so no provincial tax is payable on the first $368,000 of an eligible home. Toronto's municipal land transfer tax does not apply here.

Sources: Ontario.ca, Calculating Land Transfer Tax (updated April 22, 2026); Ontario.ca, Land Transfer Tax Refunds for First-Time Homebuyers (updated February 10, 2026). Longer local walkthrough: the first-time buyers guide.
