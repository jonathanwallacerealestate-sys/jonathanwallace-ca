Canada's Economy Is Losing Steam. Here's What It Means for Georgian Bay Buyers and Sellers

By Jonathan Wallace, REALTOR®, Faris Team Real Estate Brokerage · Updated November 2026 · ~6 min read

Thinking of selling? Book a home evaluation

The short answer: Canada's economy is slowing faster than expected, and Ontario is feeling it most. Payroll jobs fell by nearly 50,000 in August, the biggest one-month drop since 2021, and almost all of those losses were in Ontario. The economy did not grow at all in August, and more households are falling behind on their debts. For Georgian Bay, that means more cautious buyers, more motivated sellers, and a market where preparation and pricing matter more than headlines.

Key takeaways
  • Canada lost about 49,600 payroll jobs in August 2026, and Ontario accounted for roughly 44,000 of them.
  • Real GDP was flat in August, with an early estimate of a 0.1% decline in September.
  • About 11% of GTA private mortgage borrowers are now more than 90 days behind, up from about 3% in late 2023.
  • The Bank of Canada still raised its key rate to 2.50% on October 28, 2026, so plan for rates staying higher for a while.
  • For Georgian Bay buyers, a slower market means less competition. For sellers, pricing to recent local sales matters more than ever.

The Economy in Three Numbers

Here is what the latest data shows, broken down step by step.

  1. Jobs are disappearing. Payroll employment fell by about 49,600 in August, following a revised drop of 16,000 in July. Ontario lost roughly 44,000 of those jobs on its own. Payroll jobs in the province are now down 0.5% from a year ago. Outside of a recession or the pandemic, that has never happened before.
  2. Growth has stalled. The economy did not grow in August, missing the early estimate of 0.2% growth, and early numbers point to a small decline in September. If that holds, it would be the first back-to-back monthly decline since late 2025.
  3. Fewer businesses are operating. The number of active businesses across the country dipped again in August. Leave out government-related industries and the private sector is shrinking slightly.

Why Ontario Is Taking the Biggest Hit

Ontario and Quebec are the provinces most exposed to the ongoing tariff disputes with the United States, and manufacturing and trade-related jobs feel that first. Public administration also led the August job losses, which matters for government towns like Ottawa.

For Simcoe County, the takeaway is practical: when Ontario paycheques get less certain, buyers across the province get more careful, including the families and retirees who move north to Midland, Penetanguishene, Tiny, Tay and Wasaga Beach.

Households Are Feeling the Squeeze

  1. Late payments are rising almost everywhere. Delinquencies are at decade highs across most types of consumer credit, from car loans to credit cards.
  2. More homeowners are falling behind. Among private mortgage lenders in the Greater Toronto Area, about 11% of borrowers are now more than 90 days late, up from roughly 3% in late 2023, according to CMHC's latest Residential Mortgage Industry Report. Many of these are interest-only loans, where borrowers have built up very little equity.
  3. The safety net is getting thinner. Private lenders have long helped struggling borrowers refinance before they fell behind at a bank. With those lenders now under pressure themselves, fewer people will get that second chance, which means more homes could come to market from owners who need to sell.

I covered what rising arrears mean for families in Missed Mortgage Payments Are Rising. Here's What It Means for Your Family.

Where Interest Rates Fit In

The Bank of Canada raised its key rate to 2.50% on October 28, focusing on the risk of future inflation from higher energy prices. With the economy this soft, that decision is being widely questioned. Markets still see a December or January increase as likely, so it is wise to plan for rates staying higher for a while, even as the economy cools. Population Growth Is Slowing Too

Canada's population is growing far more slowly than it did between 2021 and 2024. One early signal: a major wireless carrier added almost no new subscribers last quarter, which usually tracks closely with population growth. Slower population growth means less pressure on rents and prices over the next few years, especially in big-city condo markets.

What This Means If You Are Buying in Georgian Bay

  1. Expect less competition. When confidence is low, many buyers wait. That can give you more time to decide, room for conditions like a home inspection, and better leverage on price.
  2. Protect your job and your budget first. Before you shop, make sure your income is stable and your pre-approval includes a rate hold of 90 to 120 days.
  3. Leave room in your numbers. Run your budget at a rate half a point higher than today. If the purchase still works, you are buying from a position of strength. If you are weighing rate types, see Fixed or Variable? What This Fall's Mortgage Rate Gap Means for Georgian Bay Buyers.

What This Means If You Are Selling

  1. Buyers are price-sensitive right now. A home priced to recent local sales gets attention. A home priced on last year's numbers sits, and every extra week on the market makes the next price conversation harder.
  2. Presentation matters more in a slow market. Small repairs, a clean pre-listing inspection and good photos help your home stand out when there are fewer buyers looking.
  3. Plan your timing. If you can wait, use the winter to prepare for spring. If you need to move sooner, price it right from day one. A free home value estimate is a good first step.

Why the Toronto Market Matters Up Here

Many buyers moving to Georgian Bay are selling a Toronto-area home first, and that market is under real pressure.

  1. About 1 in 4 Toronto homes sold this year went for less than the owner originally paid. Once land transfer tax and selling costs are counted, it is closer to 1 in 3, according to research from Valery.
  2. Homes bought between 2021 and 2023 have taken the biggest hits, and some new condo assignments are being offered at close to half their original presale price.
  3. Toronto's rental market is showing early signs of steadying, with record leasing activity and about 25% fewer condo rentals available than a year ago, according to Urbanation.

If your move north depends on selling in the city, get a realistic value on that property before you shop here. In this economy, the order you do things in can protect thousands of dollars.

Questions People Ask

Is Canada heading into a recession?

The economy did not grow in August 2026, and early estimates point to a small decline in September. Two straight monthly declines are a warning sign, though the third quarter as a whole is still expected to show modest growth. Ontario is weaker than the national average.

How many jobs did Ontario lose in August 2026?

Ontario lost about 44,000 payroll jobs in August 2026, close to 90% of the national decline of about 49,600. Payroll employment in the province is down 0.5% from a year ago.

How does a slowing economy affect home prices in Midland and Penetanguishene?

When confidence drops, fewer buyers are active and they become more price-sensitive. That puts downward pressure on prices across Ontario. Locally, well-priced and well-presented homes continue to sell, while overpriced homes sit longer.

Is a slower economy a good time to buy a home in Georgian Bay?

It can be, if your income is secure. Fewer competing buyers can mean more negotiating room and time to make a careful decision. Get a pre-approval with a rate hold and budget for rates staying higher for a while.

Will interest rates go down because the economy is slowing?

Not right away. The Bank of Canada raised its key rate to 2.50% on October 28, 2026 because it is worried about future inflation from energy prices. A weaker economy could slow further increases, but markets still expect one more hike by early 2027.
