More Homes Sold for Less on Average. They Also Sold Faster and Closer to Asking.

Market update·Week of October 5 to 11, 2026·~4 min read
Like this article? Join the weekly newsletter

By Jonathan Wallace, REALTOR® · Week of October 5 to 11, 2026

Simcoe County recorded 154 sales in the week of October 5 to 11, 2026, at an average sale price of $685,928, with 8.2 months of inventory. It remains a buyer's market. What moved this week was speed: more homes sold, they sold in 39 days instead of 46, and they sold at 97 percent of asking.

Key takeaways

  • Sales rose 10.8 percent to 154. New listings fell 9.2 percent to 491.
  • Months of inventory eased to 8.2 from 8.6, still well above the six-month buyer's-market line.
  • Days on market fell to 39, the lowest of the last six weeks.
  • The sales-to-new-listing ratio rose 5 points to 31 percent, and homes sold at 97 percent of asking, up a point.
  • The average sale price fell 5.0 percent to $685,928. That reflects which homes sold, not a 5 percent drop in values.

The real read

The easy headline this week is "average price down 5 percent." Read alone, it sounds like values slipped. The rest of the week says otherwise.

A weekly average moves with which homes happened to sell. This week, 154 homes sold, 15 more than the week before, and the average landed at $685,928, down from $721,684. More sales at a lower average usually means a larger share of sales came from the lower-priced end of the market, not that the same homes got cheaper.

The signals that track value pointed the other way. Homes that sold traded at 97 percent of asking, up a point. Days on market fell to 39, from 46 the week before and 56 in mid-September. If buyers were paying meaningfully less for comparable homes, those two numbers would be moving in the opposite direction.

One note on continuity. Last week's post reported 131 sales and 8.9 months of inventory for September 28 to October 4. The board's figures for that week have since been updated to 139 sales and 8.6 months, and this week's changes are measured against those updated figures.

The supply side

New listings fell 9.2 percent to 491. Fewer sellers came on just as more buyers acted, and the sales-to-new-listing ratio rose 5 points to 31 percent. Roughly one new listing in three found a buyer this week.

Here's the math on what's sitting. 491 homes came on and 154 sold. On their own, those two numbers would have added about 337 homes to the board. Instead, active listings fell by 7 to 5,230. That means roughly 344 listings left the board this week without selling. Some expired, some were cancelled, and some owners decided to wait for spring.

That combination, more sales, fewer new listings and steady withdrawals, is why months of inventory eased to 8.2. It is the lowest reading since early September, when it was 8.3. It's still a long way above six.

The bigger picture

Across the country, the fall market is uneven. BNN Bloomberg reported on October 7 on an RBC Economics report finding that most local boards saw resales drop in September, including Toronto, Hamilton, Kitchener-Waterloo and Ottawa. In the Toronto area, resales fell 5.2 percent and the benchmark price 0.5 percent from August. RBC's Robert Hogue wrote that trade tensions and interest rate concerns "put buyers on the defensive," and that the road ahead "is likely to be bumpy and uneven across markets."

That's the contrast worth noting. Our week ran a little firmer than the national picture. I wouldn't read one week as a turn. What happens in Toronto usually shows up on Georgian Bay 30 to 60 days later, and a softer GTA September suggests buyers coming north this fall will stay careful into November.

A buyer's market doesn't mean sellers lose

With 5,230 homes on the board and 8.2 months of inventory, buyers have choice and time. Yet 154 of them bought this week, in 39 days on average, at 97 percent of asking.

That's the split this market keeps showing. Homes priced to recent sales and presented well are selling, and selling faster. Homes priced to spring expectations sit, and a few hundred a week leave the board without selling. Buyers aren't refusing to buy. They're refusing to overpay.

What this means for you

Buying: 8.2 months of inventory still gives you room to negotiate, but the well-priced homes are moving faster than they were a month ago. Have your financing ready so you can act on the right home, and use the long-listed ones for leverage. Ask how long each has really been on the market, including any earlier listing.

Selling: Fewer competing new listings came on this week, and the homes that sold went closer to asking. That only helps if your price matches the last 30 to 60 days of sales. Price and present it for this fall's buyer, not last spring's.

Common questions about this market

Is it a buyer's market in Simcoe County right now?

Yes. At 8.2 months of inventory for the week of October 5 to 11, 2026, buyers hold the advantage. Above six months is the usual buyer's-market line. Months of inventory has come down from a recent high of 8.9, but it is still well above six.

Did home prices drop 5 percent in one week?

No. The average sale price fell 5.0 percent to $685,928, but a weekly average moves with which homes happened to sell. The steadier read is the list-to-sale ratio, which rose a point to 97 percent, and days on market, which fell to 39. Those don't point to buyers paying less for comparable homes.

Why did active listings barely move when 491 homes came on?

Because listings also left the board without selling. 491 homes came on and 154 sold, which on its own would have added about 337 to the active count. Instead, active listings fell by 7 to 5,230. That works out to roughly 344 listings that expired, were cancelled or were pulled this week.

How long are homes taking to sell in Simcoe County?

Homes that sold this week traded in 39 days, the lowest reading in the last six weeks, down from 46 the week before and 56 in mid-September. Days on market doesn't show price cuts, or whether the seller cancelled and relisted. Every cancel-and-relist restarts the clock at zero. If you're comparing a sold home on your street, ask how long it was listed in total, and whether it was listed earlier at a higher price.

Does what is happening across Canada matter for Midland, Penetanguishene, Tiny and Tay?

Yes. It sets the backdrop. BNN Bloomberg reported on October 7, 2026, on an RBC Economics report finding that most local boards saw resales drop in September, including Toronto, where resales fell 5.2 percent and the benchmark price 0.5 percent from August. In our market, Toronto shifts usually arrive 30 to 60 days later, so a careful GTA buyer this fall is likely a careful buyer on Georgian Bay through November.
