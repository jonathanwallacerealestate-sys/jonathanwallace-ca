Book a pre-listing inspection

By Jonathan Wallace, REALTOR® with Faris Team Real Estate Brokerage

Last updated October 12, 2026 · Midland · Penetanguishene · Tiny · Tay · Wasaga Beach

What's my home worth?

Book a pre-listing home inspection in October or November. It finds the deficiencies on your schedule, not the buyer's, and gives you the whole winter to fix them, price for them or disclose them up front. Otherwise you hear about them first from the buyer's inspector, when they get used to negotiate your price down.

Is a pre-listing home inspection worth it?

Book a pre-listing home inspection. This is the big one. An inspection now means you find the deficiencies on your schedule, not the buyer's, and you have all winter to deal with them. The things you can't fix, you can price for or disclose up front.

How to do it

  1. Book a home inspector in the fall, before the freeze.
  2. Sort the report into fix now, get quotes, and disclose.
  3. Work through the fix list over the winter.
  4. Get three written quotes for anything you can't afford to fix.
  5. Put the report, invoices and quotes in your house book.

Questions sellers ask

Is a pre-listing home inspection worth it?

In my opinion, yes, especially if you're listing in the spring. An inspection now gives you the whole winter to fix, price for or disclose what it finds, instead of hearing about it for the first time from the buyer's inspector, when it gets used to negotiate your price down.

What if the inspection finds something I can't afford to fix?

Get three written quotes before you list. Buyers almost always guess high, and with three quotes in hand you can answer with real numbers.

Do I still have to disclose something I know about?

Yes. Sellers in Ontario have to disclose latent defects. Talk to me about disclosure early. It's much easier to handle up front.
