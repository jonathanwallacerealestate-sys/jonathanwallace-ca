Seven Straight Rate Holds. Here's What the National Coverage Is Saying.

Georgian Bay market·Rate decision, September 2, 2026·~5 min read

By Jonathan Wallace · Rate decision, September 2, 2026

The Bank of Canada held its key interest rate at 2.25 percent on September 2, 2026, the seventh consecutive hold since its last cut on October 29, 2025. Governor Tiff Macklem pointed to rising oil prices from the Middle East conflict and new Canada-U.S. tariffs as the biggest risks to inflation, even as Canada's economy grew 3.3 percent annualized last quarter. Mortgage costs stay flat, for now.

Key takeaways

  • Seventh straight hold. The rate has sat at 2.25 percent since October 2025, unchanged at every meeting since.
  • Oil, not tariffs, is the bigger worry. Governor Macklem said the Iran conflict's effect on oil prices is a larger inflation risk than the new tariffs.
  • Retaliatory tariffs landed the same week. Canada's dollar-for-dollar tariffs on $27.6 billion of U.S. goods took effect days before the decision, with a further slate of measures set for September 8.
  • The economy is growing. GDP expanded 3.3 percent annualized last quarter, and inflation sits at 3 percent, above the Bank's 2 percent target.
  • Economists are split on what comes next. Desjardins and Capital Economics now expect a hike as soon as December 2026 or the first half of 2027. Rosenberg Research and KPMG expect the Bank to hold well into 2027, with any move more likely to be a cut. CIBC isn't calling it either way.

Six headlines, one decision

Before the analysis, here is how six national outlets put it in front of their own readers Wednesday morning. Same decision, six different framings.

  1. CBC News: "Inflation risks rise with higher fuel costs and new U.S. tariffs, Bank of Canada governor warns"
  2. Global News: "Bank of Canada holds key rate at 2.25 per cent once again"
  3. CTV News (Canadian Press wire): "Bank of Canada holds key rate at 2.25% as tariffs, Iran war cloud outlook"
  4. Toronto Star: "Bank of Canada holds key rate at 2.25% as tariffs, Iran war cloud outlook"
  5. Toronto Sun: "Bank of Canada holds key interest rate at 2.25%"
  6. Financial Post (National Post): "'Hawkish' Bank of Canada has some economists pulling forward calls for rate hikes"

Five of the six led with the hold itself, treating it as the expected, unremarkable outcome it was. Financial Post is the outlier, and its headline is the tell: while everyone else reported what happened, Financial Post reported what economists think happens next. That's where the real story in this week's coverage is, so that's where we go now.

What CBC reported

CBC News led with the inflation risk, not the hold itself, which nobody expected to change. Governor Macklem told reporters in Ottawa that the counter-tariffs "will add costs for some businesses," but called the bigger issue the war in the Middle East: "the longer that drags on, the bigger the risk that it starts to spill over to the prices of other goods and services." Oil prices are up roughly 13 percent since the Bank's July meeting, and Canada's inflation rate rose to 3 percent in July, above the Bank's 2 percent target.

What Global News added

Global News' coverage put more weight on the trade file. The United States imposed 50 percent tariffs on a range of Canadian goods on August 22, and Canada's matching dollar-for-dollar tariffs on $27.6 billion of U.S. goods took effect the same week as the decision, with a further slate of retaliatory measures set for September 8. Macklem's framing, as Global reported it: "Monetary policy cannot offset the effects of tariffs or influence global energy prices. What we can do is ensure global developments don't jeopardize price stability in Canada." Global also noted the economy's 3.3 percent annualized growth last quarter, though few analysts expect that pace to hold into the current quarter.

What the wire carried

CTV News, the Toronto Star, and the Toronto Sun all ran the same Canadian Press wire story, which is why their headlines above read almost identically. It's efficient, accurate reporting, and it means three of the six headlines are really one story told three times.

What the forecasters think comes next

The decision itself was unanimous among economists going in. A Reuters poll taken August 22 found all 35 participants surveyed expected the hold. What they disagree on is what happens at the next meeting, October 28, and beyond. Financial Post gathered reactions from four firms, and the spread is real, not just wire-service consensus.

Economist / firm Call
Royce Mendes
Desjardins		 Hold through year-end, then a 50 basis point hike to 2.75 percent in the first half of 2027. An accelerated call from his prior forecast of two smaller hikes.
Stephen Brown
Capital Economics		 Reads the Bank's statement as more hawkish, pointing to language that changed from rates "remain appropriate" to the Bank being ready to adjust as needed. Moved his hike call up to December 2026 from June 2027.
David Rosenberg
Rosenberg Research		 The dovish outlier. Expects a hold for some time to come, citing weak labour demand and tightening financial conditions, with the bias leaning toward an eventual cut rather than a hike.
Ali Jaffery
KPMG Economics		 Expects the Bank to stay on hold all the way until the end of 2027. If it moves at all before then, he thinks the odds favour a cut, since growth risks still outweigh inflation risks.
Avery Shenfeld
CIBC		 Declined to commit either way, writing that trade uncertainty is too clouded right now "to be definitive about what lies ahead."

Two firms now lean toward the next move being a hike, one leans toward a cut, one expects nothing to change through 2027, and CIBC isn't calling it. That range is the real story of this week's coverage: the hold was never in question, but the direction of the next move is genuinely unsettled.

What this means for Georgian Bay

Financing costs have now sat still for close to a year, which is real, useful stability for anyone budgeting a purchase in Midland, Penetanguishene, Tiny, Tay, or Wasaga Beach. But the assumption a lot of buyers have been carrying, that the next move in rates is down, is no longer the consensus it looked like a few months ago. Two of the five forecasters above now expect the next move to be a hike, not a cut, as soon as this December. Waiting indefinitely for a better rate stops being the obviously safe play. If you have a mortgage renewing in the next year, this is a good week to look at your options rather than assume next year looks like this year.

Frequently asked questions

Did the Bank of Canada raise interest rates in September 2026?

No. The Bank of Canada held its key interest rate at 2.25 percent on September 2, 2026, the seventh consecutive hold since its last cut on October 29, 2025.

How many times has the Bank of Canada held its rate since its last cut?

Seven times. The Bank of Canada has held its policy rate at 2.25 percent at every meeting since its last cut on October 29, 2025, most recently on September 2, 2026.

Could the Bank of Canada raise rates at its next meeting?

Some economists think it is possible, though there is no consensus. National coverage of the September 2, 2026 decision described Governor Tiff Macklem's tone as hawkish, with rising oil prices from the Middle East conflict and new Canada-U.S. tariffs cited as inflation risks. The Bank's next scheduled announcement is October 28, 2026.

Are economists predicting the Bank of Canada's next move will be a hike or a cut?

They disagree. Desjardins and Capital Economics now expect a rate hike, as soon as December 2026 by Capital Economics' estimate. Rosenberg Research and KPMG Economics expect the Bank to hold well into 2027, and see a cut as more likely than a hike if the rate does move. CIBC has not committed to a direction either way.
