Should I list my house "as is, where is"?

Jonathan Wallace, REALTOR® · 0:46 video

The short answer

Usually not. Those four words sound safe, but buyers start picturing leaks or foundation problems and offer less, some walk away thinking you're hiding something, and the buyer's bank can start asking questions too. If something doesn't work, like the dishwasher, name just that one thing so buyers don't invent problems that don't exist.

What Jonathan Wallace said

Four words in your listing that can make buyers think the absolute worst: as is, where is. It means you're selling the home exactly as it sits, with no fixes. It sounds safe, but here's what it really does.

Buyers see those words and they start picturing leaks or foundation problems, and they offer you less money. Some get so nervous that they walk away completely, thinking that you're hiding something. And in my experience, the buyer's bank can notice it too, and they start asking what's wrong. So all that from a few words meant to keep things easy.

Here's the better way. If something doesn't work, like the dishwasher, name just that one thing, so that you don't have buyers inventing problems that don't exist.
