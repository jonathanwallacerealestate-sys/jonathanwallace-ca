Who owns the beach? A guide to shoreline ownership in Tiny Township

Waterfront guide · Updated August 2026 · ~7 min read

The short answer: In Tiny Township, the beach in front of a cottage can be First Nation, provincial, township, communal, privately owned to the water, or still unresolved in the record. Jonathan Wallace treats that as a sale question first: what can you dock, what can you fence, and what will a buyer finance.

Key takeaways

  1. Tiny Township lists six ownership pictures. Naming which one applies is the whole job.
  2. The 66-foot Crown shore road allowance is the single most common source of confusion. Two neighbouring cottages can hold different rights to the same beach.
  3. Deeded beach access and beach ownership are separate questions with separate answers.
  4. Use and a fence are not title. Confirm with the deed, the registry, and a survey before you list or buy.
  5. Tiny, Midland, and Penetanguishene are not NVCA municipalities. On a Tiny lot the shoreline stack is the Township, MNR, and DFO.

Jonathan Wallace is asked this more than any other shoreline question in Tiny Township. Where does the lot end, and who owns the sand between the cottage and Georgian Bay?

Owners assume they know. Buyers assume the fence line is the answer. Two houses on the same stretch can hold different rights to the beach in front of them. That is not a curiosity. It is what a lender, an insurer, and a buyer will underwrite before a waterfront deal closes.

This page is how Jonathan Wallace reads that picture on a Tiny listing or offer. It is general information, not legal advice. Title is confirmed by a lawyer, a surveyor, the Land Registry Office in Barrie, and the Township of Tiny, not by a blog.

Six ways a Tiny shoreline can be owned

The Township of Tiny lists six ownership pictures. Naming which one applies is the whole job.

  1. First Nation to the water's edge, as at Christian Island.
  2. Provincial, as at Awenda Provincial Park.
  3. Township-owned. That includes stretches of Woodland Beach, Bluewater Beach, Jackson Park Beach, Balm Beach, and Lafontaine Beach, and other municipal land.
  4. Communal, as at Rowntree Beach, where a defined group of owners shares the land.
  5. Individual private ownership to the water.
  6. Unknown, because the patent and the later transfers do not resolve cleanly.

That last one surprises people. A lot of this shore was surveyed and patented more than a century ago. The record is not always a modern PIN with a neat line.

Tiny's own explainer is here: Beach and shoreline ownership. Read it. Then have the lot read against it.

The 66-foot strip that trips the sale

Most of the confusion is the original Crown shore road allowance.

When these townships were laid out in the 1800s, surveyors often reserved a strip, typically 66 feet (one chain), inland from the high-water mark. The point then was public passage along the water. That use is gone. The strip is still in the title.

On one lot, a prior owner closed and bought that allowance, so the deed runs toward the water. On the lot next door, the 66-foot strip is still public, even if it has been mowed and furnished like a yard. Those two cottages do not sell as the same product.

If the allowance is still public, clearing, landscaping, or putting a structure on it needs municipal permission. In-water work is a separate stack: the Township, the Ministry of Natural Resources (Public Lands Act), and Fisheries and Oceans Canada. Tiny, Midland, and Penetanguishene are not in the Nottawasaga Valley Conservation Authority. Wasaga Beach is. Do not call NVCA for a Tiny lot.

Some original Crown patents, including those issued to the surveyor John Goessman in 1823, reserved one chain (about 66 feet) as a road "with free access to the beach of all vessels, boats and persons." On August 6, 2025, Tiny Council passed By-law 25-045 by a vote of 4-1 to accept dedication of those reserves on certain broken township lots along Georgian Bay. The Township's position is that the by-law affirms jurisdiction and public access, not new parking or development. Council had unanimously rejected assuming these same reserves in 2002, citing surveying uncertainty and administrative complexity, which is why the 2025 vote drew debate. The file is not finished: Council received legal advice in closed session in January 2026 and the matter has not been concluded. If the lot sits on one of the affected reserves, Jonathan Wallace wants the current by-law, the survey, and the lawyer's reading in the file before anyone prices the sand as private. Confirm the status with the Township of Tiny the week you list or offer.

What this does to a sale

A buyer who thinks they own to the water, and later learns the sand is township or communal, does not only get upset. They lose a dock plan, a fence plan, or a financing condition. A seller who lists "private beach" on a public or communal strip creates a claim. A lender who cannot see a clear line to the water prices the file as a different asset.

Before photos go up, confirm whether any interim control by-law or shoreline restriction applies to the lot. A private line on paper does not help if you cannot touch the shore. Check with Tiny directly.

Deeded access is a different question

Who owns the sand is one question. Who may cross it is another.

Many interior lots were sold with deeded beach access: a right of way over land the owner does not own. Tiny will not confirm that right for you. The Township says it does not verify title or easements for deeded access to any private property. That answer is in the deed, with a lawyer.

If access is why someone is buying, do not waive that condition.

Why this keeps ending up in court

Tiny's own record points to Plan 779, Block B of Plan 656 at Woodland Beach (Court of Appeal), and the original road allowance between Concessions 18 and 19. The reported cases include Tiny v. Battaglia and Ellard v. Tiny.

The lesson is not that every beach is a fight. The lesson is that use and a fence are not title. Write it down.

What is happening at the Township right now

As of August 2026, three files are active.

The Goessman reserves described above remain an open file. By-law 25-045 accepted dedication of the 1823 Crown reservations on certain broken lots. Residents on both sides have organized. The opposition has cited the 2002 rejection, the possibility that the 66-foot reservations follow the 1823 shoreline (which may place portions of them underwater today), surveying uncertainty, and cost. Council received solicitor-client advice in January 2026. The matter has not been concluded.

Separately, the Interim Control By-law (23-044) remains in force. No new structures, fences, boathouses, retaining walls, patios, decks, landscaping, or site alteration near the 178-metre contour are permitted while the study is underway. The May 2025 shoreline by-laws, including Official Plan Amendment 5, zoning by-law 25-039 establishing a Shoreline Development Area, and shoreline by-law 25-040 covering site alteration, fences, and retaining walls, have all been appealed to the Ontario Land Tribunal. Until those appeals are resolved, Interim Control By-law 23-044 is the operating rule. Do not assume the 2025 permit system is in effect. For current information, contact the Township at interimcontrol@tiny.ca or 705-526-4204.

None of these files are settled. Candidates in the October 2026 municipal election will have positions on them. Understanding the baseline helps you evaluate those positions and, if you are buying or selling, understand what applies to the lot you are looking at right now.

How Jonathan Wallace confirms it on a listing

  1. Ask Tiny whether that stretch is municipally owned. Many township beaches are signed.
  2. Pull the parcel and any road allowance at the Land Registry Office in Barrie.
  3. Put an Ontario Land Surveyor on the ground if the line is the price.
  4. Have a real estate lawyer read title, easements, and any deeded access.
  5. Check for any shoreline freeze, outstanding by-law, or septic order before the sign goes in.

For a buyer, those items go in the conditions. Do not waive them on Tiny waterfront because the sand "has always been used this way."

For a seller, do this before the photos. The first weekend of showings is the wrong time to learn the beach is not in the deed.

Frequently asked questions

Who owns the beach in Tiny Township?

It depends on the lot. Jonathan Wallace starts with Tiny Township's six ownership pictures: First Nation, provincial, township, communal, private to the water, or unknown. The answer is in the deed and the survey, not the fence.

Do I own to the water's edge in Tiny?

Only if title says so. Many lots in Tiny Township stop at a 66-foot shore road allowance or a municipal or communal block. Neighbouring cottages can differ.

What is the 66-foot shore road allowance?

A one-chain strip reserved in the original Crown surveys along Georgian Bay. Some owners later bought it. Some did not. If it is still public, work on it needs Tiny Township's permission, and in-water work needs MNR and DFO approvals where those rules apply.

Is this an NVCA permit?

Not in Tiny Township, Midland, or Penetanguishene. Those municipalities are not NVCA members. Wasaga Beach is. On a Tiny lot the shoreline stack is the Township, MNR, and DFO.

Can I replace a dock if I do not own the beach?

Ownership of the sand and permission to put a structure in the water are different files. New crib docks may sit outside the current DFO code of practice. Do not assume a wrecked crib can be rebuilt as-is. Confirm with DFO before planning.

Is there a building freeze on Tiny's shoreline?

Yes. Interim Control By-law 23-044 is in force as of August 2026. No new structures, fences, boathouses, retaining walls, patios, decks, landscaping, or site alteration near the 178-metre contour are permitted. The May 2025 shoreline by-laws have been appealed to the Ontario Land Tribunal. Until those appeals are resolved, the interim control by-law is the operating rule. Contact the Township at interimcontrol@tiny.ca for current status.

What about a one-foot Crown reserve?

That is a provincial tool on some public-land dispositions, not one of Tiny Township's six beach categories. What owners on this shore actually encounter is the 66-foot allowance, a signed municipal beach, a communal block, or a private line to the water.

I inherited the cottage. Where do I start?

Start with title and the survey, then decide keep, sell, or buy the other interests out. Jonathan Wallace can price the shoreline once the ownership picture is in writing. Tax treatment is the accountant's file.

The close

Along this shore, waterfront is the lifestyle. It is not automatically the title. The beach in front of a Tiny Township cottage might be in your name at the water, a public allowance, a communal block, or a record that still will not resolve. The only way to know is the deed, the registry, and the survey.
