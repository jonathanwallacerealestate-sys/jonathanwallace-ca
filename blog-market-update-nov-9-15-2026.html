Sales Fell 25 Percent. So Why Did the Market Get Tighter?

Market update·Week of November 9 to 15, 2026·~4 min read
Thinking of selling? Get Your Personalized Home Value Report

By Jonathan Wallace, REALTOR® · Week of November 9 to 15, 2026

The quick read this week is easy: fewer sales, lower prices, a slower market. Ninety-nine homes sold across Simcoe County between November 9 and 15, down from 132 the week before. But the more useful story is in the listings.

Key takeaways

  • 99 homes sold, down 25.0% from 132 the week before.
  • 332 new listings came on, the lowest weekly total since January.
  • Months of inventory eased to 8.5. It's still a buyer's market, just slightly less so.
  • The average price was $693,942, down 9.9%. That's mostly a change in which homes sold.
  • Homes averaged 59 days on market. That's the fourth weekly increase in a row.

The real read

Sellers pulled back faster than buyers did. New listings fell 14.7% to 332, the lowest weekly total since January. Active listings dropped by 113 to 4,684. Supply shrank along with sales, so months of inventory went down to 8.5 from 8.6 instead of climbing.

That's what November usually looks like. Many sellers would rather wait for spring than show a home through the holidays, so fresh supply thins out. At 8.5 months the market still favours buyers, since anything above six months counts as a buyer's market. But the gap stopped widening this week.

About that price drop

Last week's average sale price, with late-reported sales included, was $770,236. This week it's $693,942. Neither number tells you what your home is worth. With fewer than 100 sales in a week, a few higher-end or waterfront sales landing one week and not the next can swing the average sharply.

The list-to-sale ratio is the steadier signal. It was 95%, down one point, so buyers are still paying about 95 cents of every asking dollar.

The bigger picture

CREA released its October numbers this week. The Canadian Press reported that 37,827 homes sold across Canada, down 10.6% from October 2025. On a seasonally adjusted basis, sales edged up 0.3% from September. New listings rose 0.8% and outpaced sales. CREA's chair called it only a slight slowdown from summer to fall, even though interest rates rose in late October. Simcoe County is following the same pattern, just with more inventory on hand.

What I'm seeing on the ground

The gap between what sellers expect and what buyers will pay is starting to close. A big reason is history. Most neighbourhoods now have enough recent sales that a seller can see exactly where prices have gone, and realtors can point to real numbers, including the declines, instead of hopes.

Sellers are less interested in setting a record on their street. Most now understand that if they want to sell in this market, the price has to be realistic. A home priced too high doesn't just sit. The longer it sits, the more buyers start to wonder what's wrong with it.

There are good deals out there for buyers. For sellers, the picture depends on when you bought. If you bought in the 1990s, you are still walking away with a very healthy gain. If you bought in 2020 or later, it is very hard to come out ahead right now.

My honest read, from the deals I'm working on: the market isn't likely to change much in the next 18 to 24 months, and it may get a little softer before it gets better. Waiting for spring can feel like betting red or black at the roulette table: maybe prices go up, maybe they go down, even odds. It isn't. With the trend pointing down, betting on a spring rebound is more like putting your chips on a single number on the inside of the board. It can hit, but the odds are against you, and guessing wrong could cost you tens of thousands of dollars. If this sale is how you plan to cash out and move forward with your life, now is the time.

What this means for you

Buying: You have deep selection, with 4,684 active listings, and room to negotiate price, conditions and closing dates. Winter buyers face less competition, and sellers listing now tend to have a real reason to move.

Selling: You're competing with fewer new listings than at any point since January. Price to the last 90 days of comparable sales, not to last spring. If you're weighing now against spring, I'm glad to look at the numbers for your street in Midland, Penetanguishene, Tiny, Tay or Wasaga Beach.

Common questions about this market

Is it a buyer's market in Midland and Simcoe County right now?

Yes. Simcoe County, which includes Midland, Penetanguishene, Tiny, Tay and Wasaga Beach, had 8.5 months of inventory for the week of November 9 to 15, 2026. Anything above six months is a buyer's market. It eased slightly from 8.6 the week before because new listings fell to 332, the fewest since January.

Should I sell my house in Midland now or wait until spring?

If you're ready to move, selling now is usually the better bet in Midland and across Simcoe County. Competition is lower this winter, with new listings at their lowest since January, and the trend doesn't point to higher prices by spring. Waiting is a bet that prices recover, and guessing wrong could cost tens of thousands of dollars. The exception is a home that needs work to show well; fix that first.

Are home prices dropping in Simcoe County?

Prices are soft, but one week's number overstates it. The Simcoe County average sale price was $693,942 for November 9 to 15, 2026, down 9.9% on the week, and that weekly average swings with which homes happen to sell. Province-wide, CREA forecasts Ontario's average price to fall about 3% in 2026. Homes are still selling at about 95% of asking.

Will home prices in Midland and Wasaga Beach go up in 2027?

CREA expects Ontario home sales to rise about 5.3% in 2027 after a weaker 2026, and CMHC expects prices to recover only slowly. From what I'm seeing in Midland, Wasaga Beach and the rest of Simcoe County, the market is unlikely to change much over the next 18 to 24 months and may soften a little before it improves.

How does the Bank of Canada rate hike affect home buyers and sellers in Simcoe County?

The Bank of Canada raised its policy rate to 2.50% on October 28, 2026, its first increase since 2023. Variable mortgage rates rose right away. Fixed rates follow bond yields rather than the Bank's rate, but they were already climbing. Higher borrowing costs trim what some Simcoe County buyers can spend, which keeps pressure on prices. If you're buying, get pre-approved and ask about a rate hold.

How long does it take to sell a house in Midland or Wasaga Beach right now?

Across Simcoe County, which includes Midland and Wasaga Beach, homes that sold the week of November 9 to 15, 2026 averaged 59 days on market, the fourth weekly increase in a row. Days on market resets when a listing is cancelled and relisted, so ask how long a home has really been for sale.

Is now a good time to buy a house in Midland, Penetanguishene or Wasaga Beach?

For buyers who are ready, yes. Simcoe County has 4,684 active listings, homes are selling at about 95% of asking, and winter brings less competition in Midland, Penetanguishene and Wasaga Beach. Sellers listing now usually have a real reason to move, which makes them easier to negotiate with. With rates rising, get pre-approved before you shop.
