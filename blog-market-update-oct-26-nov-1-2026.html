Sellers Stepped Back for Winter. The Homes That Stayed On Still Sold.

Market update·Week of October 26 to November 1, 2026·~5 min read
Thinking of selling? Get Your Personalized Home Value Report

By Jonathan Wallace, REALTOR® · Week of October 26 to November 1, 2026

Simcoe County recorded 149 sales in the week of October 26 to November 1, 2026, at an average sale price of $730,014, with 8.4 months of inventory. It remains a buyer's market. The story this week is not buyers. It is sellers stepping back for the winter, the way they do every year at the end of October.

Key takeaways

  • Sales held steady at 149, up 0.7 percent.
  • The average sale price fell 8.1 percent to $730,014. One week's average moves with which homes happened to close, so read it as a change in the mix, not a drop in values.
  • New listings fell 14.0 percent to 386. Active listings fell by 350 to 4,860.
  • Months of inventory eased 0.8 to 8.4. The sales-to-new-listing ratio rose 6 points to 39 percent.
  • Days on market rose 10 to 50. Homes sold at 95 percent of asking, down 1 point.

The real read

Months of inventory fell to 8.4, and at first glance that looks like the market tightening. It isn't, at least not the way people mean it.

Buyers did not suddenly show up. Sales were flat, up just 0.7 percent to 149. What changed is the other side of the ledger. Every year, the end of October is when homes come off the market. Owners who do not want to sell through the winter pull their listings and plan to try again in spring. That is what this week's numbers show.

The supply side

Here's the math. 386 new listings came on and 149 homes sold, which on its own would have added about 237 homes to the board. Instead, active listings fell by 350 to 4,860. That means roughly 587 listings left the market without selling, through expiries, cancellations and owners taking a winter break.

New listings also slowed, down 14.0 percent. Fewer people are choosing to start a sale in November. Put those two together and the sales-to-new-listing ratio jumped 6 points to 39 percent: about 39 sales for every 100 new listings.

Months of inventory has now run 8.9, 8.6, 8.2, 9.2, 9.2 and 8.4 over the last six weeks. Days on market ran 49, 46, 39, 44, 40 and 50. The homes that did sell took longer and closed a little further from asking. That is not a seller's market forming. It is a quieter board with fewer choices on it.

The bigger picture

The Greater Toronto Area is seeing the same thing on a bigger scale. Global News reported on November 4 that GTA home sales in October were down 7.9 percent from a year earlier, with new listings down 11.9 percent and active listings down 10 percent. TRREB's chief information officer said GTA buyers "continued to benefit from substantial negotiating power on price over the past year."

Fewer homes for sale, and buyers who still hold the leverage. That is the backdrop around us, and it lines up with what we are seeing locally.

A buyer's market does not mean sellers lose

With 4,860 homes on the board, buyers still have plenty to look at. But 149 of them bought this week, and the homes that sold went for 95 percent of asking. A seller who stays on through the winter is competing with fewer homes than a month ago. The buyers shopping in November are usually the serious ones.

The catch is days on market. At 50 days, buyers have time to compare. A home that is priced to recent nearby sales and shows well is the one they pick. A home priced off a spring number is the one they skip.

What this means for you

Buying: 8.4 months of inventory still means time and choice. There are fewer listings than a month ago, so if a home fits, don't wait for spring to see it. The owners who stayed on through November usually want to sell. Ask how long a listing has really been for sale, including any earlier listing, because a cancel and relist resets the days-on-market clock.

Selling: If you were planning to pull your home for the winter, it's worth a second look. Less competition is real. So is the patience of today's buyers. Price against what nearby homes actually sold for recently, not the county average, and make sure the home shows well in November light.

Common questions about this market

Is it a buyer's market in Simcoe County right now?

Yes. Months of inventory was 8.4 for the week of October 26 to November 1, 2026, down 0.8. Anything above six months is a buyer's market.

Why did months of inventory drop this week?

Mostly because sellers pulled their homes off the market for the winter. Sales held steady at 149, up 0.7 percent, while active listings fell by 350 to 4,860. Roughly 587 listings left without selling. Fewer homes on the board brings months of inventory down even when buyers are not more active.

Did home prices fall 8 percent in Simcoe County?

No. The average sale price was $730,014, down 8.1 percent on the week. A single week's average moves with which homes happened to close, so it tracks the mix of sales, not a county-wide drop in values. Homes sold at 95 percent of asking, down 1 point.

How long are homes taking to sell in Simcoe County?

Homes that sold in the week of October 26 to November 1, 2026 took 50 days on average, up 10 days. Days on market resets when a listing is cancelled and relisted, so ask how long a home has really been for sale.

Should I take my home off the market for the winter?

Not automatically. Many sellers pull their homes at the end of October, so the ones that stay on face less competition. Buyers shopping in November tend to be serious. If your home is priced to recent nearby sales and shows well, staying on can work in your favour.
