Fewer Homes, Fewer Sales, Higher Prices. So Who Has the Edge?

Market update·Week of November 2 to 8, 2026·~4 min read
Thinking of selling? Get Your Personalized Home Value Report

By Jonathan Wallace, REALTOR® · Week of November 2 to 8, 2026

Simcoe County recorded 126 sales in the week of November 2 to 8, 2026, at an average sale price of $764,017, with 8.6 months of inventory. At first glance it looks like a contradiction: inventory fell and prices rose. It isn't the market tightening. It is a buyer's market getting slightly stronger.

Key takeaways

  • Sales fell 15.4 percent to 126, from 149 the week before.
  • The average sale price rose 4.7 percent to $764,017. One week's average moves with which homes happened to close, so read it as a change in the mix, not a jump in values.
  • Active listings fell by 75 to 4,785. New listings edged up 0.8 percent to 389.
  • Months of inventory rose from 8.4 to 8.6. The sales-to-new-listing ratio fell 7 points to 32 percent.
  • Days on market rose 1 to 51. Homes sold at 95 percent of asking, unchanged from last week.

The real read

Sales dropped 15.4 percent to 126 homes. Active listings fell by 75. Buyers stepped back faster than supply did, so months of inventory went up, from 8.4 to 8.6. Fewer homes sold, but the homes still for sale would take even longer to clear.

That is a buyer's market getting slightly stronger, not weaker.

About that price jump

The average sale price rose 4.7 percent to $764,017. In a slower week, that number moves easily with the mix of homes that sold. A few higher-end closings can lift the average without any home becoming worth more.

The list-to-sale ratio tells you more: it held at 95 percent. Buyers are still negotiating.

The bigger picture

The latest Ontario numbers show the same pattern across the province. OREA and CREA reported that October sales were down 8.7 percent from last year, and active listings remain well above the 10-year average.

Ontario sits at 4.9 months of inventory. Simcoe County is at 8.6. Our region is leaning further toward buyers than the province as a whole.

A buyer's market does not mean sellers lose

Homes are taking longer to sell, with 51 days on market on average. But the drop in active listings means a well-priced, well-presented home faces less competition than it did a month ago.

In this market, sellers who price from the data sell. Sellers who price from hope wait.

What this means for you

Buying: you have time, choice and room to negotiate. Use all three, especially before the spring market brings buyers back.

Selling: your price and presentation now decide your result. The first two weeks on market matter more than ever.

Common questions about this market

Is it a buyer's market in Simcoe County right now?

Yes. Months of inventory was 8.6 for the week of November 2 to 8, 2026, up from 8.4 the week before. Anything above six months is a buyer's market.

Why did months of inventory rise when active listings fell?

Because sales fell faster than supply. Sales dropped 15.4 percent to 126 while active listings fell by 75 to 4,785. With fewer homes selling each week, the homes still for sale would take longer to clear.

Did home prices go up 4.7 percent in Simcoe County?

No. The average sale price was $764,017, up 4.7 percent on the week. In a slower week, the average moves with which homes happened to close, so a few higher-end sales can lift it. Homes sold at 95 percent of asking, unchanged on the week, so buyers are still negotiating.

How long are homes taking to sell in Simcoe County?

Homes that sold in the week of November 2 to 8, 2026 took 51 days on average, up 1 day. Days on market resets when a listing is cancelled and relisted, so ask how long a home has really been for sale.

How does Simcoe County compare with the rest of Ontario?

Simcoe County is leaning further toward buyers. Ontario finished October at 4.9 months of inventory, according to OREA and CREA. Simcoe County was at 8.6 for the week of November 2 to 8, 2026.
