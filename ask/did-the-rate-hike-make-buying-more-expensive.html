Straight Answers · Buying

Did the rate hike make buying a home in Simcoe County more expensive?

Jonathan Wallace, REALTOR® · 1:26 video

The short answer

Not necessarily. Your payment depends on the price and the rate, and Simcoe County prices are down about 3% in a year. On a $550,000 home with 5% down, the lower price saves about $90 a month while the higher rate adds back about $75, so the payment is still a little lower than last fall. You can always get a better rate later. You can never go back and change the price you paid.

What Jonathan Wallace said

The Bank of Canada just raised its rates by a quarter point, but buying a home here in Simcoe County actually just got marginally cheaper, and here's how it works. Your monthly payment depends on two things: the price of the home and the rate of your loan. When prices drop, you borrow less, and borrowing less can make up for that higher rate.

Now picture Sam and Jess. They're out looking at their first home this week for $550,000, and they find a house that they absolutely love. Then the news hits: rates are going up. Their first thought is, we just missed our chance. So we sit down and we run the numbers.

Prices in Simcoe County have dropped about 3% in a year, so that same house last fall would have cost them about $16,000 more. With a 5% down payment, that lower price saves them about $90 a month. Now, that higher rate does add back about $75 a month. So even after the increase, their payment is a little bit lower than it would have been last fall. And over a 25-year period, that lower price saves them about $26,000.

Now, nobody knows the bottom until prices start rising again, and buying isn't for everybody. But if you're in a good spot to own, the math is on your side right now. You can always get a better rate down the road, and you can never go back and change the price you paid.
