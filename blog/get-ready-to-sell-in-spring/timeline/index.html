Your fall-to-spring timeline

By Jonathan Wallace, REALTOR® with Faris Team Real Estate Brokerage

Last updated October 12, 2026 · Midland · Penetanguishene · Tiny · Tay · Wasaga Beach

Your fall-to-spring timeline

October to November · before the freeze

Outside work and the big unknowns

  • Fall exterior and drone photos. (See fall photos.) If you have waterfront, photograph the shoreline and the dock before they come out or freeze in.
  • Book a pre-listing home inspection. This is the big one. An inspection now means you find the deficiencies on your schedule, not the buyer's, and you have all winter to deal with them. The things you can't fix, you can price for or disclose up front.
  • Get the septic pumped and inspected. Keep the hauler's written report. If the inside of the tank needs parging, it has to be done while temperatures are still holding above about 10°C so the cement cures properly. Check the product's own temperature requirements with your contractor. Once November turns cold, that job waits until spring, when you'd rather be listing. In Tiny, also check whether your area is part of the Township's septic re-inspection program and whether you have any open deficiency notices.
  • Test your well water. Simcoe Muskoka District Health Unit provides free bacteria testing. Pick up bottles at the Midland office, and the sample has to be tested within 48 hours (SMDHU). You'll test again after the spring melt.
  • Fix loose handrails on the deck and any wobbly steps. It's a small job, and every inspector flags it. Buyers notice right away when a railing moves under their hand.
  • Open up the attic and check for unexpected visitors. Look for droppings, nesting, chewed wiring and disturbed insulation. There's nothing worse than the buyer's inspector finding a rodent problem. In my experience, about half of buyers are instantly turned off when that happens, and the rest start second-guessing their decision. If you find something, deal with it now and keep the pest-control invoice.
  • Service the furnace and A/C if it's not too late in the season, and keep the records. It shows buyers everything has been maintained and is working.
  • Clean the gutters and winterize outdoor taps and hoses (CMHC maintenance schedule). After the first light snow, look for spots where the snow on the roof melts first. They show heat leaking into the attic, which is where ice dams start (NRCan).
  • Book a WETT inspection for any wood stove, insert or pellet stove. Many insurers require one, and if a buyer can't get insurance, they can't get financing (WETT Inc.).
  • Start any shoreline approvals now. Docks, retaining walls and beach work can need Township, conservation authority or other approvals, and none of them happen fast. See Tiny's shoreline construction page and the NVCA permit FAQ.

December to January · inside work

Work through the inspection list

  • Get rid of water stains. Here's a common one: a slow leak got fixed properly by a professional four years ago, but nobody ever painted the ceiling. Now's the time. Prime it with a stain blocker, paint it, and put the repair invoice in your house book. You're taking away the question before a buyer can ask it. (A known past issue still gets disclosed. The paint just means nobody has to wonder whether it's still leaking.)
  • Declutter room by room. Pack up what you won't need until you move. Winter is the perfect time for it.
  • Finish the small repairs: dripping taps, sticky doors, cracked caulking, burnt-out bulbs, missing cover plates.
  • Test smoke and CO alarms. You need a CO alarm on every storey and outside sleeping areas if you have a fuel-burning appliance, a fireplace or an attached garage (Ontario.ca).
  • Start your house book (see house book) and gather receipts, warranties and permits for any renovations.
  • If the place sits empty, read your insurance policy's vacancy and heating clauses. Frozen-pipe damage in a vacant home is often not covered (IBC).
  • Consider a winter radon test. A long-term test runs at least 91 days, so starting in winter means you'll have results by spring (Health Canada).

February to March · paperwork and pricing

Get the documents and the number ready

  • Find your survey. If it's old or doesn't show your current fences, additions or outbuildings, talk to me about whether an updated Surveyor's Real Property Report makes sense (AOLS).
  • Order record searches. In Tiny, you can order a septic record search for your permit history (Tiny). The ESA can tell you if there are open electrical notifications on the property, and its full history requests take about 15 business days (ESA).
  • Oil tank? Find its age, location and inspection records. Older tanks and tanks of unknown age can create insurance and legal issues, so get the facts from TSSA or a registered contractor early (O. Reg. 213/01).
  • Private road or right-of-way? Gather any road-maintenance or snowplowing agreements. Buyers and lenders will want to know how year-round access works.
  • Talk to me about disclosure. Make a list of anything you know about. Sellers in Ontario have to disclose latent defects (RECO), and it's much easier to handle up front.
  • Review pricing. In late winter, we'll go over a fresh comparative market analysis based on what's sold and what you'll be competing with.
  • Book your photographer, cleaners, stager and trades. Spring is their busy season too.

The final 2 to 4 weeks before you list

Spring walk-around, deep clean, photos

  • Walk the property and check the roof, gutters, foundation, grading and sump pump for winter damage.
  • Re-test the well after the spring melt, and if you have a cottage, open it up carefully: restore the water, check for burst lines and test the pressure tank.
  • Deep clean everything: windows, baseboards, light fixtures, grout, the inside of the appliances. A well-cared-for house sells for more. Buyers read a clean house as a well-maintained house.
  • Depersonalize and stage the main rooms, and clear the walkways of snow, ice and slush.
  • Interior photos on a bright day, paired with your fall exteriors.
  • Put the house book on the kitchen counter.

Questions sellers ask

When should I start getting my house ready to sell in the spring?

Start in October. Around Georgian Bay, the outside jobs (fall photos, septic, deck repairs, anything involving cement) have to happen before the freeze. Winter is for the inside work and the paperwork, and February and March are for pricing and booking your photographer and trades. If you wait until April, you're doing everything at once, in the mud.

What happens if I wait until April?

The lot is wet, the snowbanks are grey, and every contractor in North Simcoe is booked. You end up doing everything at once.

When should I book my photographer and trades?

In February and March. Spring is their busy season too.
