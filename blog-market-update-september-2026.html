September 2026 Brought the Listings Back. Buyers Did Not Come With Them.

Monthly market update·September 2026 in review·~6 min read
Thinking of selling? Book a home evaluation

By Jonathan Wallace, REALTOR® · September 2026 in review

Simcoe County recorded 658 sales in September 2026 at an average sale price of $750,436, with 7.99 months of inventory. It remains a buyer's market. September did what September always does and brought sellers back. New listings jumped 16.4 percent. The buyers did not follow. Sales fell 6.8 percent from August and 17.3 percent from September 2025.

Key takeaways

  • New listings rose 16.4 percent to 2,365 while sales fell 6.8 percent to 658.
  • Months of inventory climbed to 7.99, from 7.60 in August and 6.85 in September 2025.
  • The average sale price rose 3.5 percent to $750,436, but it is still 3.3 percent below last September.
  • Days on market rose to 49, from 45 in August and 44 a year earlier.
  • Every Georgian Bay market except Orillia sold fewer homes than in August. Severn was the only one to beat its September 2025 count.

The real read

More sellers listed in September than in August. That increase did not bring more completed sales. In August, roughly 35 of every 100 new listings in the county found a buyer. In September it was about 28.

Active listings actually fell 2.0 percent, to 5,257. So this was not a flood of supply. Months of inventory went up because sales went down, not because the pile of homes for sale grew. That is a demand story.

The average price rising 3.5 percent does not change that. The list to sale price ratio held almost flat at 96.8 percent, up 0.1 points. Buyers did not suddenly start paying closer to asking. The average alone does not show whether individual homes gained value. A different group of homes sold each month.

The supply story

2,365 new listings came on in September, 333 more than in August. That is still 12.9 percent fewer than September 2025, so sellers were not rushing to the exits. They were doing what they do every fall: listing before the weather turns.

For anyone who listed in September, the competition was the backlog, not the new arrivals. 5,257 homes were sitting for sale at month end. At September's pace, that is close to eight months of supply.

Midland and Penetanguishene

The sharpest slowdown on the Bay. Sales fell to 35 from 46 in August and 48 a year earlier. Homes that sold averaged 74 days on market, up from 48, the longest monthly reading in the past year.

New listings rose 25.8 percent to 117 while active listings dipped to 270, so months of inventory jumped to 7.71 from 6.15. The average price rose 13.1 percent to $601,166, but homes sold at 95.5 percent of asking, down from 96.3. On 35 sales, a few larger homes can move the average a long way. The slower pace is the real signal.

Tay Township and Tiny Township

The deepest buyer's market in our part of the Bay at 12.05 months of inventory, up from 9.41 last September. Days on market jumped to 65 from 48. Homes sold at 94.2 percent of asking, the lowest ratio of the six markets. On a home at the area's $728,696 average, that gap is roughly $42,000.

Active listings fell 8.2 percent to 470, and new listings eased rather than rising, so sellers here are not adding pressure. Buyers simply have time.

Wasaga Beach

Mixed. Sales fell 11.8 percent to 45, and months of inventory rose to 11.04, well above 7.38 a year ago. But the homes that sold moved faster, at 46 days compared with 55 in August, and sold at 97.2 percent of asking, second only to Orillia. The 29.2 percent jump in the average price to $722,109 says more about which homes sold than about values.

Severn

The only Georgian Bay market to sell more homes than last September: 24, up from 21. Sales did dip from August's 26, and days on market rose to 48 from 33. The average price of $909,813 is up 22.1 percent on the month, from 24 sales, so treat it with care.

Collingwood and The Blue Mountains

The biggest supply jump of the six. New listings rose 46.6 percent to 305 while sales slipped to 56, pushing months of inventory to 13.41, the highest of the six markets. Sales were down 36.4 percent from September 2025. This market spans Simcoe County and Grey County, so it is not counted inside the county totals.

Orillia

The exception. Sales rose 3.7 percent to 56, homes sold in 36 days, down from 53, and the list to sale price ratio was 97.3 percent. At 4.79 months of inventory, Orillia is the only one of the six below the six-month buyer's market line. The 22.3 percent drop in its average price to $538,898 came alongside faster, firmer sales, which points to which homes sold rather than falling values.

A buyer's market does not mean sellers lose

Wasaga Beach and Orillia show it. In both, the homes that sold went faster and closer to asking than they did in August. A well-priced, well-presented home still finds a buyer in this market. What does not work is pricing off last spring and waiting for the market to come to you.

What this means for you

Buying: September added 2,365 fresh listings on top of a backlog of more than 5,000 homes. In Tay Township and Tiny Township, homes are selling at about 94 percent of asking after two months on the market, so there is room to negotiate on price, conditions and closing dates.

Selling: the fall wave means more homes are competing for fewer buyers. Price to the last 60 to 90 days of sales in your own community, not to the county average and not to a neighbour's sale from last spring. In Midland and Penetanguishene, expect a longer runway than you would have a year ago.

Common questions about this market

Was Simcoe County a buyer's market in September 2026?

Yes. The county finished September 2026 at 7.99 months of inventory, up from 7.60 in August and 6.85 in September 2025. Anything above six months is a buyer's market.

How long were homes taking to sell in Midland and Penetanguishene in September 2026?

Homes that sold averaged 74 days on market, up from 48 in August and 55 in September 2025. When a listing is cancelled and put back on the market, the days on market count starts over, so some homes have been for sale longer than the number shows.

Did home prices go up in Georgian Bay in September 2026?

Averages rose in Midland and Penetanguishene, Wasaga Beach, Severn and Collingwood, and fell in Tay and Tiny and in Orillia. With 24 to 56 sales per market, the average moves with which homes sold. It is not a measure of what any one home is worth.

Which Georgian Bay market was the strongest in September 2026?

Orillia. It was the only one of the six where sales rose from August, and the only one under six months of inventory, at 4.79. Severn was the only market that sold more homes than in September 2025.

What should sellers in Tay Township or Tiny Township expect?

A longer sale and real negotiation. In September 2026 the area had 12.05 months of inventory, homes took 65 days to sell, and they sold at 94.2 percent of asking.
