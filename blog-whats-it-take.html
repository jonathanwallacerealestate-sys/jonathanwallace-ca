Market timing

Is now a good time to buy or sell on Georgian Bay?

Georgian Bay market · July 2026 · ~7 min read

It is the question I get more than any other right now, from both sides of the deal. Is this a good time to buy? Is this a good time to sell? The honest answer is that timing has less to do with the calendar than with your own situation. But there are real patterns in this market worth understanding before you decide. Here is my straight read on the Georgian Bay and Simcoe County market this summer, and how I would think about it if it were my own move.

First, what summer actually looks like here

July slows down. People are at the beach, at the cottage, away for a week or two, and buying a house is the last thing on their mind. That is normal for this region every year. What follows is just as predictable. A surge tends to arrive in late August, as families push to get settled before the school year, and that momentum carries through September and October. Most sales close in about 45 to 60 days, so a lot of buyers this fall are aiming to be in before the holidays.

None of that means the market is broken. It means the market is seasonal, the way it always has been here.

The real cost of waiting for spring

The most common instinct I hear from sellers is to wait for the spring market. Sometimes that is the right call. But it is worth doing the math first. Across the region, average sale prices sit between roughly $500,000 and $700,000. Over the past few years, prices have softened at a rate of about $3,000 to $5,000 a month depending on the location.

So if you believe the market keeps easing before it turns, every month your home is not on the market can cost you $3,000 to $6,000 in value. Waiting is not free. It is a decision with a price attached, and it is worth knowing that number before you make it.

One thing steadying the picture: the Bank of Canada has now held its rate six times in a row. That kind of stability pulls people off the sidelines, because they can plan around a life change instead of guessing at a spreadsheet.

What about first-time buyers?

This is the group I would tell to pay closest attention. Entry-level homes in the area start around $375,000. At today's rates, near 3.99 to 4.1% on a five-year fixed and 3.6 to 3.7% on a variable, the monthly payment on a starter home is often close to, or below, what the same people are paying in rent for a comparable apartment.

Over a long horizon, these properties have appreciated in the range of 4 to 5% a year. If you are buying with a five to ten year view, buying in a softer, better-supplied market is usually a stronger position than chasing one that is already running. For the right first-time buyer, this is a window, not a warning.

List in August, or wait for September?

The answer depends less on the month and more on what you are competing against. A home listed when fewer comparable properties are on the market has better odds, full stop. A compelling price, a strong presentation, and genuine value still get a home sold, in August or September. So the real question is not which month. It is how your home stacks up against everything else a buyer is looking at that week.

A word on cottages and second properties

The secondary property market moves to its own rhythm. Purchases have declined over the last few years, and an aging ownership base plus rising carrying costs are pushing more of these homes onto the market as sellers. If you bought a recreational property in the last decade, you may be looking at a softer number than you paid. If you have owned longer, you are likely still sitting on real appreciation. Either way, pricing a cottage today takes a clear-eyed look at recent local sales, not a number from the peak.

Thinking of selling? Start here

If selling is on your mind, the best light of the year is right now. Summer is when your gardens, your lawn, and your exterior photograph at their best, and nearly every buyer meets your home online before they ever meet it in person.

Buyers fall in love with curb appeal. Most people choose a home on how it makes them feel, and that feeling starts at the curb. Before showings begin, it pays to remove the small things a buyer could hold against you, because every perceived issue becomes a reason to offer less. For most sellers, that preparation takes about 7 to 10 days.

My listing playbook

When you list with me, the plan is the same one that gets homes sold in any season. We build the strategy together, then do a staging consultation with preliminary photography so we know exactly how the home should present. From there it is comprehensive professional photography, two to three hours including drone, floor plans, and 3D walkthroughs, and a full marketing launch across the syndicated platforms within 48 hours. The goal is simple: your home shows at its absolute best, everywhere a buyer is looking, from day one.

The big picture, and the biggest mistake

Step back and the numbers are clear. Simcoe County is carrying about 7.8 months of inventory, and Tiny Township is closer to 11. That is a buyer's market. But quality homes, priced and presented well, are still selling quickly inside it.

The biggest mistake I see in a summer like this is underestimating the market. Overpricing to start, missing the momentum, then chasing the price down through the fall. What began as a plan to sell in 30 to 60 days becomes 180 days on the market, and a lower final number than an honest price would have brought on day one.

Bottom line

Is now a good time to buy or sell? For the right person, with the right plan, yes. Sellers do best with a full-time agent and a proven track record behind the marketing. Buyers do best with an agent watching inventory closely and ready to move when the right home appears. Quality homes still sell quickly, even in a buyer-friendly market. The first real step is a straight conversation about your situation, not a guess at the calendar.
