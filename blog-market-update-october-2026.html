Simcoe County Sold 14 Percent Fewer Homes Than Last October. In Midland and Penetanguishene, Homes Sold a Month Faster.

Monthly market update·October 2026 in review·~6 min read
Thinking of selling? Book a home evaluation

By Jonathan Wallace, REALTOR® · October 2026 in review

Simcoe County recorded 632 sales in October 2026 at an average sale price of $721,479, with 7.69 months of inventory. It remains a buyer's market. Months of inventory improved from 7.99 in September, but it sits 10.4 percent above October 2025, when the county was at 6.96.

Key takeaways

  • Months of inventory fell to 7.69 from 7.99 in September, and is up 10.4 percent from October 2025.
  • Sales fell 4.0 percent from September and 14.4 percent from October 2025.
  • New listings fell 16.1 percent on the month, the largest move in the county data. Active listings fell 7.6 percent.
  • Days on market dropped to 42 from 49. The list to sale price ratio slipped to 96.4 percent.
  • Around Georgian Bay, four of the five markets improved their months of inventory, and four of the five sold below the county's list to sale price ratio.

Read October against September and it looks like the market turned a corner. Fewer homes sitting, faster sales, a lower inventory number.

Read it against last October and the picture reverses.

The real read

In July, the year over year numbers were the encouraging ones. Sales were running ahead of 2025 while supply shrank, and months of inventory was lower than a year earlier.

October flipped that. Against October 2025, Simcoe County sold 632 homes instead of 738, a 14.4 percent drop, while active listings fell only 5.4 percent, from 5,138 to 4,858. Demand fell faster than supply, and months of inventory is now higher than it was a year ago.

The month over month improvement came almost entirely from the seller side. New listings fell by 380 and active listings fell by 399 from September. Sales fell by 26. Sellers took homes off the market or chose not to list before winter. Buyers did not suddenly absorb more inventory.

Days on market dropped from 49 to 42, and part of that is who left. When long-sitting listings are withdrawn instead of sold, they never enter the days on market average. The homes that did sell were mostly the ones priced to the market from the start.

The list to sale price ratio slipped to 96.4 percent. On a home at the county average, that is roughly $27,000 below asking. Buyers are negotiating a little harder than they were in September.

The supply story

New listings came in at 1,985, down 16.1 percent from September and 13.5 percent from October 2025. Some of that is the season. Fall listings thin out every year as owners decide to wait for spring.

For anyone who lists in November or December, that means fewer new homes arriving to compete with yours. It does not mean more buyers. Those are two separate things, and October showed both numbers falling at once.

The bigger picture

October's sales were almost all written before the Bank of Canada raised its overnight rate to 2.5 percent on October 28, in the same stretch that Ontario led the country's biggest job losses since 2021.

That makes November the first full month that shows how buyers respond to higher borrowing costs. October tells you where the market stood going into that decision, not after it.

Midland and Penetanguishene

The fastest change in the region. Homes that sold averaged 44 days on market, down 40.5 percent from roughly 74 days in September. That is about a month faster.

Supply did the rest. New listings fell 32.5 percent to 79, the largest pullback of the five markets, and active listings fell 12.6 percent to 236. Sales held close to September at 33, so months of inventory improved to 7.15.

The average price fell 9.6 percent to $543,274. With 33 sales, a few lower priced homes can move that number a long way, so read it as what sold rather than what any one home is worth. Homes sold at 95.8 percent of asking.

Tay Township and Tiny Township

Still one of the deepest buyer's markets on the Bay at 10.87 months of inventory, but improving. Active listings fell 12.1 percent to 413 while sales held at 38, and days on market dropped to 46 from roughly 65.

The number to watch is 95.0 percent, the lowest list to sale price ratio of the five markets. On a home at the area's $661,576 average, that is roughly $35,000 of negotiation. Buyers here have time, and they are using it.

Wasaga Beach

The biggest inventory improvement of the month. Months of inventory fell 19.9 percent to 8.85 as active listings dropped by 81 and sales rose slightly to 47.

In July, Wasaga Beach hit 11.93 months of inventory after a sharp drop in sales, and the honest read then was to watch it before pricing against it. Three months later it sits at 8.85. The average price fell 11.1 percent to $641,623, and days on market rose to 51.

Collingwood and The Blue Mountains

More buyers, and they moved faster. Sales rose 10.7 percent to 62 and days on market fell to 56 from roughly 69. The average price eased 4.7 percent to $948,595.

At 11.90 months of inventory, this is still the deepest buyer's market of the five. New listings fell 22.3 percent, but active listings barely moved, so the backlog here is clearing slowly. This market spans Simcoe County and Grey County, so it is not counted inside the county totals above.

Orillia

The only one of the five where months of inventory rose, to 5.33 from 4.79. Sales fell 19.6 percent to 45 and days on market rose to 43.

Even so, Orillia remains the tightest market in this report and the closest to balanced. Its 96.5 percent list to sale price ratio was the strongest of the five, and the average price rose 9.5 percent to $589,929.

What the Bay did differently from the county

Three of the five Georgian Bay markets sold faster in October, and four of the five saw months of inventory improve. On the surface, the Bay had a better month than the county headline.

The list to sale price ratio tells the rest. Barrie sold at 97.4 percent of asking. The Georgian Bay markets sold between 95.0 and 96.5 percent. Buyers here have more choice than buyers in Barrie, and they are negotiating accordingly.

That gap matters more than the average price line. A home priced to recent sales in its own community still sells. A home priced to a county figure, or to a neighbour's sale from last spring, waits.

What this means for you

Buying: you have selection and room to negotiate, especially in Tay Township, Tiny Township, Collingwood and The Blue Mountains, where inventory sits near 11 to 12 months. The October 28 rate increase changes what your budget buys, so bring your pre-approval up to date before you start booking showings.

Selling: new listings fell sharply across the Bay in October, so a home that comes out in November or December faces less fresh competition. Buyers are still negotiating four to five points off asking. Price to the last 30 to 60 days of sales in your specific community, and do the preparation before the sign goes up, not after the first price reduction.

If you are weighing a move around Midland, Penetanguishene, Tiny Township, Tay Township or Wasaga Beach this winter, this market works for people with a plan.

Common questions about this market

Is it a buyer's market in Simcoe County right now?

Yes. Simcoe County finished October 2026 at 7.69 months of inventory. Anything above six months is generally considered a buyer's market. That figure improved from 7.99 in September but is 10.4 percent higher than October 2025, so buyers have more selection than they did a year ago.

How long are homes taking to sell in Simcoe County?

Homes in Simcoe County averaged 42 days on market in October 2026, down from 49 in September and 43 in October 2025. Around Georgian Bay, Midland and Penetanguishene averaged 44 days, down from roughly 74 in September, while Collingwood and The Blue Mountains averaged 56.

Are home prices going up or down in Georgian Bay?

The Simcoe County average sale price was $721,479 in October 2026, down 3.9 percent from September and 8.3 percent from October 2025. That average tracks what sold rather than what any one home is worth. Four of the five Georgian Bay markets saw their average fall in October, while Orillia rose 9.5 percent to $589,929.

What should sellers in Tay Township or Tiny Township do in this market?

Price to recent local sales and expect negotiation. Tay Township and Tiny Township sat at 10.87 months of inventory in October 2026, with homes selling at 95.0 percent of asking, the lowest ratio of the five Georgian Bay markets. Days on market fell to 46, so well-priced homes are moving.
