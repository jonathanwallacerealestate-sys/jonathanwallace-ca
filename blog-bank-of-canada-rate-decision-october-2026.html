Will the Bank of Canada Raise Rates This Week? What the Data Says for Georgian Bay Buyers and Sellers

By Jonathan Wallace, REALTOR, Faris Team Real Estate Brokerage · Updated October 2026 · ~6 min read

Like this article? Join the weekly newsletter

The short answer: Heading into the Bank of Canada's rate decision on Wednesday, October 28, financial markets are betting on roughly an 80% chance of a quarter-point hike. The latest data makes a stronger case for a hold than that number suggests: core inflation just fell to its lowest level in six years, and Canada has lost about 110,000 jobs in two months. Whatever happens Wednesday, the bigger story for Georgian Bay is that fixed mortgage rates are already priced as if several hikes are coming.

What the Market Is Expecting on Wednesday

Bond markets are pricing in a 25 basis point increase this week, followed by roughly three more before next summer. That is an aggressive path. If you have been watching fixed rates climb this fall, this expectation is the reason: lenders set fixed rates off bond yields, and bond yields move on what investors think the Bank will do, not what it has done.

The question for anyone buying, selling or renewing in Midland, Penetanguishene, Tiny, Tay or Wasaga Beach is simple. Is that expectation justified, or is the market getting ahead of itself?

Three Reasons the Data Points to a Hold

  1. Core inflation is cooling, not heating. September's headline inflation came in at 3.1%, slightly below the 3.2% forecasters expected, and gasoline alone accounted for about 1.1 percentage points of it. Strip out the volatile items and the Bank's preferred core measures dropped to 1.9%, the lowest reading in six years. Small businesses are also planning smaller price increases: the CFIB's October survey showed expected price hikes falling to 2.8% from 3.3%.
  2. Inflation expectations are holding steady. The Bank watches closely for signs that Canadians expect prices to keep climbing, because that belief can feed on itself. Consumer expectations two years out actually edged down to 3.9% from 4.0%, and business expectations also came in lower. The one warning light is longer-term consumer expectations, which jumped to 3.9% from 3.4%, a four-year high. That series swings a lot from quarter to quarter, so one reading is worth watching rather than acting on.
  3. The job market is weakening fast. Canada has shed roughly 110,000 jobs over the past two months, and the CFIB's measure of full-time hiring plans fell to its lowest level since 2020. Raising rates into a softening job market is a hard case to make. It is also worth remembering that today's price pressure is coming largely from supply, like oil and refining, and higher interest rates do very little to fix supply problems.

None of this guarantees a hold. The Bank may still move. But the odds look a lot closer to a coin flip than to 80%, and a pause until December or January would be a reasonable outcome.

What This Means If You Are Buying in Georgian Bay

  1. Understand what you are paying for with a fixed rate. Five-year fixed rates are sitting about 1.0 to 1.2 percentage points above variable rates. In practical terms, a fixed rate today already assumes four or five more Bank of Canada hikes. If fewer hikes arrive, variable borrowers come out ahead. I walked through what that gap means in monthly dollars in Fixed or Variable? What This Fall's Mortgage Rate Gap Means for Georgian Bay Buyers.
  2. Get your pre-approval rate held now. Most lenders will hold a rate for 90 to 120 days. If Wednesday brings a hike, a hold protects you. If it brings a pause, you can usually take the lower rate if one becomes available.
  3. Decide based on your own comfort, not the headlines. A variable rate rewards people who can absorb a payment increase without losing sleep. A fixed rate buys certainty, and certainty has real value when you are stretching for a waterfront property or a first home. Your mortgage professional can run both scenarios on your actual numbers.

What This Means If You Are Selling

Rate uncertainty tends to slow decisions, not stop them. Buyers who are already qualified are still moving; they are simply more careful about price. A home that is priced to the current market and presented well continues to sell here. A home priced on hope sits longer, and every week on the market in a rate-uncertain fall makes the next price conversation harder.

If you are planning a spring sale, this is the season to prepare: get repairs and inspections handled now, and price with the most recent sales, not last year's.

The Condo and New Home Picture, and Why It Matters Up Here

Many of the buyers moving to Georgian Bay are selling a condo or townhome in the Toronto area first. What is happening in that market directly shapes their budget here.

  1. New condo sales hit a record low. September new condo sales in the Toronto region fell about 40% from a year ago, the weakest September on record. Some resale condo units are now trading at roughly a third below their pre-pandemic prices.
  2. New low-rise homes are moving again. Ontario's HST relief on new homes helped drive low-rise sales up about 126% year over year in September, led by townhouses. Asking prices on unsold new low-rise inventory have fallen back to around 2016 levels. That is a sign first-time buyers may be re-entering the market.
  3. Toronto condo rents have flattened. Active condo rental listings fell 29% from a year ago in September, and the average signed condo lease held at about $2,654 a month, essentially unchanged. With record purpose-built rental supply still coming, that stability may not last.

For a buyer coming north, the takeaway is practical: if your plan depends on selling a Toronto-area condo, get a realistic value on it before you fall in love with a property in Tiny or Penetanguishene. The sequence matters more this year than it has in a long time.

What Household Finances Are Telling Us

  1. Mortgage growth has slowed to 3.8% a year, matching the lowest pace of this cycle. Canadians are borrowing more cautiously for homes.
  2. Credit card balances are rising quickly, up 6.5% year over year, the fastest annual pace since late 2024. Personal bank deposits continue to shrink.
  3. There is good news too. The share of Canadians who expect to miss a debt payment in the next three months fell to its lowest level since 2024.
  4. The economy is splitting in two. Retail sales are up a healthy 5.4% from a year ago, while monthly food bank visits have reached 2.35 million, up about 140% from before the pandemic. Some households are spending comfortably and others are under real strain.

That split shows up in real estate too. Well-funded buyers are still active, especially for lifestyle and waterfront properties. Buyers stretching to qualify are feeling every rate move.

Questions People Ask

When is the next Bank of Canada rate decision?

The next announcement is Wednesday, October 28, 2026. The following decision is scheduled for December.

Will the Bank of Canada raise rates on October 28, 2026?

Markets were pricing roughly an 80% chance of a quarter-point hike heading into the decision. The data makes a stronger case for a hold than that: core inflation fell to 1.9%, a six-year low, and Canada lost about 110,000 jobs over two months.

Will mortgage rates go up if the Bank of Canada holds?

Not necessarily. Variable rates move with the Bank's policy rate, so a hold keeps them where they are. Fixed rates follow bond yields, which already reflect expected hikes. If the Bank holds and signals patience, fixed rates could ease.

Is a fixed or variable mortgage better right now?

It depends on your budget and your tolerance for payment changes. Fixed rates sit about 1.0 to 1.2 percentage points above variable, which means they already assume several future hikes. Speak with a mortgage professional who can model both options on your numbers.

How do Toronto condo prices affect Georgian Bay home buyers?

Many buyers relocating to Georgian Bay fund their purchase by selling a Toronto-area condo. With some resale condos trading well below pre-pandemic prices, those buyers have smaller budgets than they expected, which affects what they can offer here.

Is now a good time to sell a home in Midland or Penetanguishene?

Well-priced, well-presented homes continue to sell. Rate uncertainty makes buyers more price-sensitive, so pricing to recent sales matters more than usual. A local market review will show you where your home sits today.
