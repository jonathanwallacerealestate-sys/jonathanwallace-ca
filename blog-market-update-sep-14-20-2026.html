Sales Rose 13 Percent This Week. Buyers Still Gained More Ground.

Market update·Week of September 14 to 20, 2026·~3 min read

By Jonathan Wallace, REALTOR® · Week of September 14 to 20, 2026

Simcoe County recorded 147 sales in the week of September 14 to 20, 2026, at an average sale price of $711,967, with 8.9 months of inventory. It remains a buyer's market. Sales picked up this week, and buyers still came out of it with more leverage than they went in with.

Key takeaways

  • Months of inventory rose 0.6 to 8.9, the highest reading in six weeks and well above the six-month buyer's-market line.
  • Sales rose 13.1 percent to 147, but 600 new listings arrived, up 41, and active listings grew by 71 to 5,407.
  • About one new listing in four found a buyer. The sales-to-new-listing ratio improved 2 points to 25 percent.
  • The average sale price fell 7.6 percent to $711,967, a shift in which homes sold more than a change in what homes are worth.
  • Homes that sold took about 57 days, 11 more than the week before, and still traded at 96 percent of asking.
  • In the GTA, new single-family home sales more than tripled in August from a year earlier as the HST rebate on new homes pulled buyers back (Toronto Star). Resale sellers here compete with builders as well as with each other.

The real read

More homes sold this week. 147 sales is a 13.1 percent jump, and after two soft weeks, that is real buyer activity.

The listing side moved faster. 600 new listings came on, 41 more than the week before, and active inventory grew by 71 to 5,407. Months of inventory rose 0.6 to 8.9.

That is the week in one line: buyers showed up, and sellers showed up in greater numbers.

The average sale price fell 7.6 percent to $711,967. That does not mean homes lost 7.6 percent of their value in seven days. A busier week changes which homes sold, and the average follows the mix. Read it with the sales count, not instead of it.

The supply side

For the second week in a row, the fall listing push outran buyers. At a 25 percent sales-to-new-listing ratio, three of every four new listings joined the board rather than leaving it.

With 5,407 homes available and 8.9 months of inventory, buyers are not short of options. They are short of reasons to rush.

The bigger picture

The busiest corner of the Ontario market right now is not resale. The Toronto Star reported this week that new single-family home sales in the GTA more than tripled in August from a year earlier, according to Altus Group data prepared for the building industry. The Building Industry and Land Development Association credits the HST rebate on new homes, brought in this spring, with offsetting the chill from U.S. tariffs.

Price is doing the rest. The benchmark price for a new single-family home in the GTA was $1.25 million in August, down 14.6 percent from a year earlier.

That matters here for two reasons. First, what happens in the GTA typically reaches Georgian Bay 30 to 60 days later in our market, and a buyer pool that is moving again in the GTA is one that eventually looks north. Second, resale sellers here are not only competing with 5,407 other listings. They are competing with builders who have a rebate to offer and prices that have already reset.

If you are buying, compare new and resale on the full cost, and ask whether a new home qualifies for the rebate. If you are selling a resale home, your price has to hold up next to both.

A buyer's market does not mean sellers lose

147 homes sold this week at 96 percent of asking. Buyers are active. They are also selective.

Homes that sold took about 57 days to get there, 11 days longer than the week before. That is the cost of landing in the middle of the pack when buyers have 5,407 homes to compare.

The homes that move are the ones that give a buyer a clear reason to choose them: priced against the last 30 to 60 days of sales, and presented better than the listings around them. Everything else waits.

What this means for you

Buying: 5,407 active listings and 8.9 months of inventory means you can take your time, compare carefully and negotiate on price and conditions. If you are weighing a new build, compare it with resale on the full cost, and ask whether it qualifies for the HST rebate.

Selling: You are competing with 600 new listings from this week alone. Price to recent sales, not to spring, and make the value obvious in the first two weeks. If your listing has been sitting, change something real before you ask the market the same question again.

Common questions about this market

Is it a buyer's market in Simcoe County right now?

Yes. At 8.9 months of inventory for the week of September 14 to 20, 2026, buyers hold the advantage. Above six months is the usual buyer's-market line on this board read, and inventory rose 0.6 on the week.

Are home prices going up or down in Georgian Bay?

The average sale price in Simcoe County was $711,967 in the week of September 14 to 20, 2026, down 7.6 percent on the week. Weekly averages move with the mix of homes that happen to sell, so the steadier read is the list to sale price ratio, which held at 96 percent. Homes priced to the market are holding their value. The softness is at the margins, where listings are priced above recent sales.

How long are homes taking to sell in Simcoe County?

Homes that sold this week traded in about 57 days, 11 days longer than the week before. That number is easy to misread.

Days on market does not track whether the home had price cuts over the last year, or whether the seller cancelled and relisted. Every cancel-and-relist starts the clock at zero again. So a home that “sold in 57 days” often sat much longer in real life. Dig into the history and you will see plenty that were on the market for 180 to 500 days before the price finally moved into a range that brought a buyer. The 57-day print is the last chapter, not the whole book.

If you are comparing a sold home on your street, ask first: how long was it listed in total, and was it listed earlier at a higher price? That is the true read on how long the property was sitting.

What should sellers in Midland or Penetanguishene do in this market?

Price to the last 30 to 60 days of evidence, not to last spring, and do not settle for the middle of the pack. With 600 new listings arriving in a single week, buyers choose the most value for the money. That is what still gets you into the 96 percent list-to-sale group.

Does the GTA new-home rebound matter for Georgian Bay?

It can. The Toronto Star reported that new single-family home sales in the GTA more than tripled in August from a year earlier, with the building industry crediting the HST rebate on new homes. What happens in the GTA typically reaches Georgian Bay 30 to 60 days later in our market. In the meantime, resale sellers here are competing with builders as well as with the 5,407 other listings on the board.
