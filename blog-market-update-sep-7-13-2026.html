Last Week's Inventory Dip Did Not Hold. New Listings Came Back Faster Than Buyers.

Market update·Week of September 7 to 13, 2026·~3 min read

By Jonathan Wallace, REALTOR® · Week of September 7 to 13, 2026

Simcoe County recorded 124 sales in the week of September 7 to 13, 2026, at an average sale price of $769,992, with 8.4 months of inventory. It remains a buyer's market. The number that genuinely moved is not the average price. It is the return of supply against a softer sales pace.

Key takeaways

  • Months of inventory rose from 7.6 to 8.4, deeper into buyer territory (above six months).
  • Sales fell to 124, down 16.8 percent from the prior week, while new listings climbed to 559, up 12.7 percent.
  • Active listings rose by 64 to 5,334. The sales-to-new-listing ratio fell 9 points to 22 percent.
  • Homes that sold still traded at 96 percent of asking in about 47 days.
  • Across Canada, August sales cooled while new listings rose, and sellers are gearing up for the fall market under trade uncertainty (Globe and Mail). Simcoe's market feels that same tension: more homes listed, fewer deals closed.

The real read

A week earlier, months of inventory looked better for the wrong reason. Spring listing agreements were ending. Buyers had not suddenly returned. This week the board reversed that pause.

Fewer homes sold. More homes came on. Active listings grew. Months of inventory moved from 7.6 to 8.4.

When sales slow and new listings rise at the same time, it takes longer to clear the board. Buyers get more choice. Sellers compete harder for the same attention. That can feel frustrating if you are living it. The math is still simple.

The average sale price rose to $769,992 even as sales fell. That does not mean every street got more expensive overnight. A quieter week changes which homes sold. Read the average with the sales count, not instead of it.

The supply side

559 new listings is a real fall push. At a 22 percent sales-to-new-listing ratio, supply came on faster than buyers were ready. Active inventory at 5,334 gives buyers room to compare and wait.

Last week's inventory dip only parked some of the competition. This week put that competition back on the page.

The bigger picture

If you have been circling a listing decision, or watching a friend do it, this part feels personal. You want to catch the fall market before the winter slowdown begins. You also do not want to list into a week when buyers go quiet. That second-guessing is normal. It is what uncertainty does when real money is on the line.

The Globe and Mail is describing the same split nationally. Some owners are pulling back. Others are gearing up for fall anyway, after late summer felt more hopeful than it turned out to be.

Simcoe showed that tension this week. 559 new listings are the people who decided to show up for fall. 124 sales are the people still waiting for clarity. When only about 22 percent of those new listings find a buyer in the same week, Georgian Bay did not run out of houses. Confidence got harder to find than inventory.

That usually reaches Midland, Penetanguishene, Tiny, Tay, and Wasaga Beach a little later than the national headline, often 30 to 60 days later. So a soft August print still matters for late September and October here. The local story is simple: more people trying to sell into fall, fewer people ready to buy, and a board that makes that frustration easy to see.

A buyer's market does not mean sellers lose

Homes that sold still traded at 96 percent of asking in about 47 days. At 8.4 months of inventory, buyers have time. Homes priced and presented to today's market can still sell on a normal timeline.

This week does not prove Simcoe County homes cannot sell. It proves the market is selective. The homes buyers want still move. The rest sit longer.

I saw that last week. I took over a listing that had been with another agent for almost a year and a half. Good people in a hard spot. They had been chasing a declining market, and every time they reset the price, they still landed in the middle of the pack.

Middle of the pack is where you disappear. Buyers are not only hunting the lowest number. They want the most value for what they have to pay. When your home blends in, you are just another option in a long scroll.

This market is rewarding motivated sellers who step out of the middle and give a buyer a clear reason to choose them. Everyone else is left waiting, revisiting plan B, or choosing patience. That is what 8.4 months of inventory feels like when your listing looks average next to a lot of other average listings.

With this one, we listed at the front of the pack. It was clearly the best value for what a buyer had to pay. We had an offer within seven days and sold in eleven, for a price they did not love but were willing to accept so they could move into the future they wanted. We did not set a record on the street. We were the next home to sell, and they got to move forward. In this market, that is the win.

What this means for you

Buying: 124 sales against 5,334 actives is a wide field. Use it. Compare condition and carrying cost. Look for the most value for the money, not only the lowest ask.

Selling: If you list this week, you are joining 559 other new attempts inside 8.4 months of inventory. Middle of the pack is how a listing goes quiet. Price and presentation have to make the value obvious. If you have already been sitting too long, change something real before you ask the market the same question again.

Common questions about this market

Is it a buyer's market in Simcoe County right now?

Yes. At 8.4 months of inventory for the week of September 7 to 13, 2026, buyers hold the advantage. Above six months is the usual buyer's-market line on this board read.

Why did the average price rise when sales fell?

The average reflects which homes closed, not a promise that every home gained value. Use sold comparables on your street, not the county average alone.

How long are homes taking to sell in Simcoe County?

Homes that sold this week traded in about 47 days, flat with the week before. That number is easy to misread.

Days on market does not track whether the home had price cuts over the last year, or whether the seller cancelled and relisted. Every cancel-and-relist starts the clock at zero again. So a home that “sold in 47 days” often sat much longer in real life. Dig into the history and you will see plenty that were on the market for 180 to 500 days before the price finally moved into a range that brought a buyer. The 47-day print is the last chapter, not the whole book.

If you are comparing a sold home on your street, ask first: how long was it listed in total, and was it listed earlier at a higher price? That is the true read on how long the property was sitting.

What should sellers in Midland or Penetanguishene do in this market?

Price to the last 30 to 60 days of evidence, not to last spring, and do not settle for the middle of the pack. Buyers choose the most value for the money. That is what still gets you into the 96 percent list-to-sale group.

Does national news matter in Tiny or Midland?

Yes, as weather, not as a copy of another market. Trade uncertainty changes confidence. Georgian Bay usually feels that 30 to 60 days later. This week's board still stands on its own: more new listings, fewer sales, and 8.4 months of inventory.

Early fall on Georgian Bay is when the next market forms.
