Penetanguishene Has More Than 1,100 New Dwellings in the Pipeline. Here's What It Means for You

By Jonathan Wallace, REALTOR®, Faris Team Real Estate Brokerage · November 20, 2026 · ~7 min read

Thinking of selling? Book a home evaluation

The short answer: Penetanguishene is planning for its biggest wave of new housing in years, with projects that could add more than 1,100 dwellings. I think it is good news for the town and for Main Street. If you own an older home, expect more competition as new homes arrive. If you are buying, you will get choices Penetang has rarely had. And none of it happens overnight: most builders are waiting for the numbers to work.

Key takeaways
  • Penetanguishene is tracking projects that could add more than 1,100 dwellings: about 815 units under review, 210 with draft approval and 162 on ready-to-build lots.
  • The mix includes detached homes, townhomes, link homes and a wave of apartment proposals.
  • Owners of homes built between the 1960s and mid-1990s, especially in the east end, should expect more competition as new homes arrive.
  • Many projects are waiting because building costs are higher than what a new home sells for today.
  • More families means more demand on schools, recreation, water and sewer, and the town has been working to get ahead of it.

What Is in Penetanguishene's Housing Pipeline

MidlandToday reported on November 12 that the town is tracking projects that could add more than 1,100 dwellings. Here is how it breaks down, step by step.

  1. Under review: applications that could add about 815 homes.
  2. Draft approved: another 210 homes, with development agreements still to be signed.
  3. Ready to build: lots for about 162 more homes.

The biggest single project is 138 Robert Street East, near the future home of Burkevale Protestant Separate School: 564 homes, made up of 283 detached homes and 281 townhouses. The town's planning director puts it five to ten years from construction. Closer in, St. Andrew's Village by Matti Homes at 1145 Fuller Avenue plans 173 dwellings, mostly detached, with rental townhouses and apartments, and could start any time now.

Then there are the apartments: about 400 proposed at 200 Main Street and 35 Thompson Road West, 156 at 18 Wright Court and 88 at 2 Hope Street. The planning director told MidlandToday she has never, in 20 years, seen this many developers want to build apartments. A property on Brunelle Sideroad has also been pledged for a 100-unit County of Simcoe housing project with affordable units, at least five or six years away.

Every Site the Town Lists as Approved

The Town of Penetanguishene keeps a public Active Developments list of its major subdivision, condominium and rezoning files. As of November 20, 2026, three sites are marked approved but not yet under construction:

  1. 123 Robert Street East: plan of subdivision and rezoning. Draft plan conditions were approved on July 12, 2023, and lapse on July 12, 2028. The original application proposed 33 townhouses on about 2.9 acres at Robert Street East, Burke Street and Lecarron Avenue.
  2. Murray Road (Maxson Subdivision): plan of subdivision and rezoning. The town list does not give a unit count.
  3. 2 Hope Street (Champlain Shores): rezoning and site plan approval for two five-storey apartment buildings with 88 units.

St. Andrew's Village at 1145 Fuller Avenue received draft plan approval in August 2020 and is now listed as under construction, along with 200 Fox Street (Champlain Shores), Harbour Pointe Phase 4 on Revol Road, 61 Thompsons Road, 77 Fox Street (Sunshine Village) and Lahey Crescent. Still in review: 138 Robert Street East, 1255 Fuller Avenue and 221 Fox Street (Queens Court, 88 condominium townhouses). For an update on a specific file, the town asks residents to contact its planning staff.

Why I Think This Is Good News for Penetang

My gut reaction was simple: Penetang needed this. Downtown Main Street has been quiet. It is a waterfront community, but from a visitor's point of view there is not a tonne of tourism, and foot traffic drops off once summer ends. More people living here year-round is what helps those businesses make it through January, not just July.

Growth also pays for things. A bigger tax base helps the town build out infrastructure and fund the waterfront plans it has talked about for years. The town has backed that up by cutting development charges by 60 percent for three years to attract builders. And it puts Penetang on the map for new builds again. The population grew 12.4 percent between 2016 and 2021, to 10,077, after years of slow growth, and a 2023 town analysis projected it could pass 13,000 by 2032.

I also think the town has been selective. Instead of nothing but large detached homes on 100-foot lots, the plan mixes detached homes, link homes, townhomes and apartments, with commercial space in the right spots. From an affordability point of view, they have done their best to appeal to as many buyers as possible. A lot is landing at once only because the town was progressive during the boom, when these applications came in.

The Apartment Wave, and What It Means for Renters

A few years ago, rentals in Midland and Penetang were very hard to find. Many of these apartment projects were pitched around 2021, when immigration was high and government incentives favoured purpose-built rentals.

The rental market looks different now. Over the last few years I have seen fewer renters applying, some weaker applications, and rents dropping by about $100 a month on average. Whether this much new supply is too much for our area is a fair question. Still, new purpose-built units beat basement apartments, and when tenants live in purpose-built rentals, fewer of them get displaced when an owner decides to sell.

Apartments and townhomes also fill a real gap for downsizers. Plenty of longtime Penetang homeowners are in houses that have become more than they want to maintain, with nothing comparable nearby to move into. These projects could finally give them somewhere to go without leaving town.

What It Means If You Own a 1960s to 1990s Home

This is the part I would pay the most attention to. If you own a single-family home built anywhere from the 1960s to the mid-1990s, especially in the east end, expect more pressure as new options arrive.

The price gap between a new home and a resale home has been getting smaller. Builders are fighting for the same buyers, so they are offering incentives, and government incentives add to that. When a wave of new inventory comes to market, resale prices nearby tend to feel some downward pressure.

  1. Know that you already have competition. There is plenty of resale inventory today, even before the new homes arrive.
  2. If you have been thinking about selling, there may be value in getting sold before that new inventory becomes available.
  3. If you want something newer and lower-maintenance yourself, the show-home stage is the time to secure a fresh model and negotiate with the builder, so you can sell your house and move into the new inventory.

What It Means If You Are Buying in Penetang

There are real opportunities on the resale side right now. But I understand the pull of a new home. Nobody has lived there before, and in some cases you can choose the finishes yourself.

Many of the new units will be smaller, and that opens the door to a different buyer. We have never had many link townhomes up here, so first-time buyers, downsizers and people who feel Barrie has outgrown them will have options in a waterfront community that did not exist before.

Why Shovels Are Not in the Ground Yet

Approved does not mean built. Builders are carrying large pieces of land, paying interest on them, and most bought with the expectation of building sooner than this. The 138 Robert Street East project will not see real progress until the economics come back.

Even St. Andrew's Village, where the infrastructure is in, has been held back by the same problem: builders cannot always build a house for what they could sell it for. As an example, if a house costs $680,000 to build and can only sell for $650,000, nobody moves forward. The economy will decide when most of this pipeline actually rolls out.

The Real Test: Schools, Recreation and Infrastructure

The biggest piece is resources. If 1,100 dwellings come to market over the next ten years, those families will need a place to swim, a place to play and schools for their kids. They will put pressure on water, sewer and town services.

Getting that right takes Town Hall staff and Council working together, so the town does not put the cart before the horse and has a plan for how these families live day to day, so they and their children can flourish here.

The good news is that the work has started. The province has put $11.3 million toward bringing the Robert Street West wells back into service as a second drinking water source, with completion targeted for 2029. The town is planning wastewater upgrades and setting money aside in reserves. A new arena and a new school are on the slate, which lines up well with the added density.

The Bottom Line

There are real opportunities coming to Penetang, and value for locals in a bigger population. It will put pressure on infrastructure, but I believe the town has done a good job of getting ahead of the in-ground work. And because Midland and Penetang lean on each other, Midland will feel the positive spinoff too.

Questions People Ask

How many new homes are planned in Penetanguishene?

Penetanguishene is tracking projects that could add more than 1,100 dwellings as of November 2026: about 815 units in applications still under review, 210 with draft approval and 162 on lots that are ready to build. They range from detached homes and townhomes to apartments.

Which housing developments are approved in Penetanguishene?

As of November 20, 2026, the Town of Penetanguishene lists three major files as approved and not yet under construction: 123 Robert Street East (plan of subdivision, draft plan conditions approved July 12, 2023), Murray Road (Maxson Subdivision, plan of subdivision) and 2 Hope Street in Champlain Shores (site plan approval for two apartment buildings with 88 units). St. Andrew's Village at 1145 Fuller Avenue was draft approved in 2020 and is now under construction.

What are the biggest housing developments in Penetanguishene?

The largest is 138 Robert Street East, with 564 homes (283 detached and 281 townhouses), about five to ten years from construction. St. Andrew's Village by Matti Homes at 1145 Fuller Avenue plans 173 dwellings and could start soon. Apartment proposals include about 400 units at 200 Main Street and 35 Thompson Road West, 156 at 18 Wright Court and 88 at 2 Hope Street.

Will new construction lower home prices in Penetanguishene?

It can put some downward pressure on resale prices, especially for homes built between the 1960s and mid-1990s in the east end, because builders are using incentives and the gap between new and resale prices has narrowed. The effect depends on how fast projects actually start, and many are waiting for the economics to work.

When will construction start on the new Penetanguishene developments?

It varies. St. Andrew's Village could start any time, while 138 Robert Street East is five to ten years out. Many builders are waiting because construction costs are higher than what a new home would sell for today, so the economy will set the pace.

Is Penetanguishene a good place to buy a home?

For many buyers, yes. Resale homes offer real opportunities in a buyer's market, and new link townhomes and apartments will add options Penetang has rarely had, including for downsizers and buyers leaving busier cities like Barrie for a more affordable waterfront community.

All answers are current as of November 2026. Pipeline totals are from the Town of Penetanguishene as reported by MidlandToday on November 12, 2026. Approval status is from the town’s Active Developments list as of November 20, 2026.
