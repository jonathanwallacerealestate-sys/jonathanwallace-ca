Seller Guide · Tay Township, Ontario

Which real estate agent should you interview in Tay Township?

By Jonathan Wallace, REALTOR®, Faris Team Real Estate Brokerage · Updated September 2026 · ~8 min read

Book an evaluation

Interview two or three agents, and make sure at least one of them actually sells in Tay Township. Most agents advertising here treat the township as one stop east of Midland. Victoria Harbour, Port McNicoll, Waubaushene, and Waverley do not price like each other, and they do not price like Midland either, even though the hospital and high school sit a short drive away.

Jonathan Wallace owns property across all four of these towns, Tay included, and follows each market closely. He lives in Tiny Township and lists across Southern Georgian Bay, including Tay's waterfront villages and the inland streets behind them. His current Tay Township listings are here, and checking what an agent actually has listed in a place is a fair way to judge whether they work there.

Below is what I would tell a friend to look for, and I say it knowing it holds me to the same standard. Four things separate agents. None of them is charm, and none of them is how long someone has had a licence.

What actually separates agents

One

Production volume, not years on a business card

Hire a full-time professional. Not someone learning current market conditions on the fly, on your listing, because they sell one or two properties a year alongside another job.

Years are a weak measure and the industry leans on them because they sound impressive. An agent with thirty-five years selling five to ten homes a year has fewer transactions behind them than an agent selling sixty-five a year for seven. The second one has seen more negotiations, more buyer behaviour, and more ways a deal goes sideways. That pattern recognition is what you are actually hiring, and it is also what buys you position and connections inside the market when it comes time to negotiate.

What matters is a proven record of winning in a challenging market, not only a rising one. Anyone looks capable when everything sells.

Ask for the numbers

How many properties did you sell last year?

What is your list-to-sale ratio?

What is your average days on market?

How many of those were in Tay Township, not just "the area"?

Two

Real money spent on marketing

Roughly ninety percent of buyers start their search online. An agent who economizes on marketing is economizing on the only place your buyer is looking.

No corners cut means all of this, included, not offered as an upgrade:

  • Professional photography
  • A 3D walkthrough
  • A 2D floor plan tour
  • A staging consultation, included in the service
  • High-quality video, not a phone walkthrough
  • Geographically targeted social posts built to pull traffic
  • Genuine syndication to third-party sites and apps, with accurate content on each one

That last point is worth pressing on. Plenty of agents will tell you a listing is syndicated everywhere when what they mean is it was cross-promoted out of REALTOR.ca and the details on half those sites are wrong or thin. Ask to see it on the actual third-party sites.

Three

They can read the data and tell you the truth about it

If a listing is not getting action, you need an agent who will say so, explain why, and articulate what repositioning it looks like. That conversation is uncomfortable and a lot of agents avoid it until the listing has gone stale.

Doing it properly requires knowing the numbers. How many showings to an offer is normal at this price point? What are days on market doing in this specific town, not the township as a whole? An agent who cannot point to those milestones does not have the big-picture read on how market data is interpreted, which means they cannot help you make a good decision about your own financial future. They can only tell you what happened, not what it means.

Ask

At my price point, how many showings usually produce an offer?

What will you tell me, and when, if we are not getting that?

Is this home being priced as Victoria Harbour, Port McNicoll, or Waubaushene, or as a township average?

Four

How fast they say yes to cutting their commission

Commission is negotiable and it is always a matter between you and the agent. This is not about the number. It is about the speed of the answer.

Agents who are genuinely successful in any market tend to have less flexibility, because they believe in what they deliver. If you ask an agent to discount and the answer is an immediate yes, the service almost always shrinks to fit the new budget, and what gets cut first is the marketing from point two.

There is a simpler version of this. If an agent cannot hold their ground on their own compensation, ask yourself how they will hold ground on the price of the largest asset you own.

Then ask the questions that are specific to Tay

The four above apply anywhere. These do not. Tay Township is a cluster of waterfront towns east of Midland. It is often the most attainable way onto Georgian Bay, and it has almost nothing in common with an in-town Midland sale ten to fifteen minutes away.

  1. How many properties have you sold in Tay Township in the last twelve months, and in which town? General Simcoe County volume is a different answer. Ask for the Tay number, then the split between Victoria Harbour, Port McNicoll, Waubaushene, and Waverley.
  2. Which town is this home in, and how does pricing here differ from the one next to it? Victoria Harbour does not price like Port McNicoll. Waubaushene does not price like Waverley. Victoria Harbour is the established core with a marina and genuine waterfront on Robbins Point Road, Hoyt, and Bayside. Port McNicoll is usually the more affordable town because it has not been built up yet. Waubaushene trades some quiet for Highway 400 convenience. A township-wide average hides all of it.
  3. Is this being priced as attainable waterfront, a water-view street, or inland Tay? Tay gives buyers a real shot at waterfront or water-view living for less than Midland or Tiny, and that is the draw. Waterfront still carries a premium over inland homes. An agent who prices everything off one township figure is not pricing at all.
  4. What is the servicing on this address: municipal water and sewer, municipal water with septic, or a well and septic? Service levels vary by town and even by street. The Tay Area Drinking Water System serves much of Victoria Harbour, Port McNicoll, and Waubaushene. Rural areas and some outskirts are still on private well and septic. Confirm it before the sign goes up. Leaving it to a buyer's conditions is how deals get messy.
  5. If this is vacant land, especially around Port McNicoll, is there a building hold until services arrive? Tay has a fair amount of vacant land. Some lots cannot be built on until municipal services reach them, which changes what a buyer can do and what the land is worth. An agent who works Tay answers that before listing. One who does not will find out from the buyer's lawyer.
  6. How will you reach the buyer coming up Highway 400, and the buyer who still needs Midland for the hospital and high school? The highway access near Waubaushene shortens the drive to Barrie and the GTA, which is why Victoria Harbour is also a popular summer-home area. At the same time, Tay looks to Midland for high school, Georgian Bay General Hospital, and larger shopping. The right agent can speak to both buyers without treating Tay as Midland's leftover inventory.

Interview two or three agents and ask all of them the same questions. The differences show up inside ten minutes.

How many homes should a real estate agent sell per year?

Well above the local average. A part-time agent selling five to ten properties a year is learning market conditions on the fly, on your listing. An agent selling well above the industry standard sees more transactions, more negotiations, and more buyer behaviour in a single year than a low-volume agent sees in five, and that pattern recognition is what you are actually hiring. Ask for last year's number specifically.

Should I hire an agent with more years of experience?

Years are a weak measure. An agent with thirty-five years selling five to ten homes a year has fewer transactions behind them than an agent selling sixty-five a year for seven years. Volume inside a defined geographic area is the better signal, and it should include a challenging market, not only a rising one. Ask for the sold count, the list-to-sale ratio, and the average days on market rather than the number of years.

What marketing should a real estate agent provide?

Roughly ninety percent of buyers begin their search online, so marketing is not a line item to economize on. Expect professional photography, a 3D walkthrough, a 2D floor plan tour, a staging consultation included in the service, high-quality video, geographically targeted social posts, and genuine syndication to third-party sites and apps with accurate content on each, rather than a feed cross-promoted from REALTOR.ca.

Should I ask an agent to lower their commission?

Commission is always negotiable and always a matter between you and the agent. What is worth paying attention to is how fast the answer comes. An immediate yes usually means the service has to shrink to fit the new budget, and the cuts land on marketing. There is also a simpler point: if an agent cannot hold their ground on their own compensation, it is fair to ask how they will hold ground on the price of your largest asset.

Do Victoria Harbour, Port McNicoll, and Waubaushene price the same?

No. Each Tay town is its own market. Victoria Harbour is the established core with a marina and waterfront on Robbins Point Road, Hoyt, and Bayside. Port McNicoll is usually the more affordable town, with railway heritage, newer development, and cottages at Paradise Point. Waubaushene sits closest to Highway 400. A township-wide average hides those differences.

Is Tay Township waterfront more attainable than Midland or Tiny?

Tay is often one of the more attainable ways onto the Georgian Bay water, which is why first-time buyers, young families, and downsizers keep looking here when Midland or Tiny waterfront stretches the budget. Waterfront still carries a premium over inland homes. Confirm current figures for the specific town and street rather than a township average.

Are homes in Tay Township on municipal water and sewer?

It depends on the address. The Tay Area Drinking Water System serves much of Victoria Harbour, Port McNicoll, and Waubaushene. In Victoria Harbour the core generally has municipal water and sewer, some outskirts have municipal water but still run on septic, and rural pockets use wells and septic entirely. Confirm servicing before you list or offer.

Can I sell vacant land in Port McNicoll?

Yes, and Tay has a fair amount of it, especially around Port McNicoll. Some lots carry building holds until municipal services reach them, which changes what a buyer can do and what the land is worth. An agent who works Tay should be able to say whether a hold applies before the sign goes up.
