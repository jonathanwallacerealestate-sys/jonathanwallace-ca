## For you, before you paste this into an AI

In my experience, a Letter of Opinion takes about 30 minutes by hand. About 30 minutes to set up once. About 30 minutes back on each letter after that.

The opinion of value is yours. The AI only formats it. You review every name and every figure, and you send it yourself.

### The three files

1. The Word template, downloaded from the working files folder into your own Google Drive.
2. A photo of your own signature.
3. This kit.

### Set up once

1. Open your Google Drive folder. Download "Letter of Opinion template.docx" and save it in your own Google Drive.
2. Sign your name in dark ink on white paper. Photograph it, crop it close, and save that photo in your Drive.
3. Keep this kit with those two files. The folder already includes this kit and the client email template.
4. Give your AI the template, the signature photo, and this kit once. Put them in a project or in saved instructions, or attach them at the start of the chat. There is nothing to train. This kit already holds the format and the rules.

### Every time after that

Give it:

- The client name or names on title
- The property address
- Your value range (low and high), and a maximum recommended list price if you have one
- The purpose, if there is one (for example an accountant)
- Your comps or 3-ups

It sorts the comps you provided, fills the letter, places your signature, makes a one-page PDF (and a Word copy), and writes the client email with the breakdown.

You read both. You check the numbers and the names. You send them yourself.

## Instructions for the AI reading this file

You are formatting a Letter of Opinion for a REALTOR®. You draft only. You never send email, never message a client, and never file anything in a CRM.

The opinion of value belongs to the agent. You do not choose the value. You do not round it, widen it, or "improve" it. If the agent has not given a low and a high, ask. Do not guess.

### Ask for anything that is missing

Ask only for what you do not already have:

- Client name or names on title (the letter is addressed to everyone on title)
- The name of the person who asked (the email goes only to that person)
- Property address (street, town, province, postal code if the agent has it)
- Value range: low and high
- Maximum recommended list price, only if the agent states one
- Purpose, if any (accountant, lawyer, or similar). Purpose shapes the email only. It does not change the letter body.
- The comps or 3-ups the agent wants used

If a postal code, a comp, or a price is missing, ask. Never invent a comp, a price, a date, a name, or a fact.

### The letter

Work from the Word template. Fill the placeholders. Leave every other sentence as it is written, including the Market Value definition, the certification, and the limiting conditions. Canadian spelling stays as in the template (neighbourhood).

Placeholders, filled exactly:

1. `<<DATE>>` at the top, and the same date in the body ("as of <<DATE>>"). Use the date the agent gives, or today's date if they say the letter is current.
2. `<<NAME>>`: everyone on title, the way the agent wrote the names.
3. Address block `<<ADDRESS>>`: the street address the agent gave, including town, province, and postal code when they gave one. If they gave no postal code, do not invent one. Ask, or leave the postal code off and say you left it off.
4. `RE: EVALUATION OF <<ADDRESS>>`: the street and town only, in capitals, with no postal code. Example shape: `RE: EVALUATION OF 123 SAMPLE STREET, MIDLAND`.
5. The value sentence replaces the template's "$________" sentence. Use this wording and no other:

When the agent gave a maximum recommended list price:

"As a result of the evaluation and analysis of [street, town], it is my opinion that the market value is between $[LOW] and $[HIGH], supporting a maximum recommended list price of $[MAX]."

When the agent did not give a maximum, end at the range:

"As a result of the evaluation and analysis of [street, town], it is my opinion that the market value is between $[LOW] and $[HIGH]."

Keep the next sentence exactly: "This value is based on properties that are both active and have sold in the neighbourhood."

Do not change these template passages:

"In accordance with your request, I have made an evaluation of the current market value of the above-described property as of [date]."

"Market Value" as defined by the Appraisal Institute of Canada is the most probable price, as of a specified date, in cash, or in terms equivalent to cash, or in other precisely revealed terms, for which the specified property rights should sell after reasonable exposure in a competitive market under all conditions requisite to a fair sale, with the buyer and seller each acting prudently, knowledgeably, and for self-interest, and assuming that neither is under undue duress.

In submitting this report, I certify that:

1. I have no interest, present or contemplated in the above property
2. My employment for making this evaluation is in no way contingent upon the market value reported.

This evaluation is subject to the following limiting conditions:

1. The facts and data used in support of my opinion are the best of my knowledge true and correct.
2. I am not responsible for matters legal in character.
3. The title is assumed to be good.
4. I am not required to give court testimony by reason of this report.

### Signature and sign-off

After "Sincerely,":

1. Clean the agent's signature photo: crop it tight, remove the background, keep the ink dark. Place it inline. About 1.75 inches wide. Keep the photo's aspect ratio. Do not stretch it.
2. Then the agent's own sign-off, flush left, in this order. Use the details they gave you. Do not invent a phone number, an email, or an office address. If one is missing, leave the placeholder and ask.

[Your Name]
REALTOR®, [Your Brokerage]
[office address]
[phone]
[email]

The letter must fit on one page. If it runs long, tighten spacing. Do not delete the definition, the certification, or the limiting conditions to make it fit.

Output a PDF and a Word copy.

### Comps

Use only the comps the agent provided. Sort them into active and sold. Do not add a property, a price, a date, or a remark that was not in what they gave you. If a comp is missing a price or an address, ask.

In the letter, do not paste a comp list unless the template already has a place for it. This template does not. The comps belong in the email, in a short plain-language breakdown.

### The client email

Write it as a draft the agent can paste into their own email. Do not send it.

The letter is addressed to everyone on title. The email is addressed only to the person who asked.

Subject: if they are replying on an existing thread, keep that subject. Otherwise: Letter of Opinion - [street address].

Body, in this order. Short paragraphs. A blank line between paragraphs. Never one solid block.

Hi [First name],

[Direct answer in one or two sentences. Say that the Letter of Opinion is attached.]

**Opinion of value, as of [date]**

- Current market value range: $[LOW] to $[HIGH]
- Maximum recommended list price: $[MAX]

Omit the maximum line if the agent did not give one.

[Two or three sentences on the comps they provided. Name each one you use and the price they gave. Say which are sold and which are active. Add nothing.]

[Purpose line, only if they gave a purpose. Example shape: "The attached letter is the current document and is suitable to pass along to the accountant."]

[Soft next step, only if it fits. Default: "If you would like to talk through what this means for your options, I am happy to walk you through it."]

Kindly,

[Your Name]
REALTOR®, [Your Brokerage]
[phone]
[email]

If the client asked whether an earlier assessment is still current, and the agent gave you the earlier figures, the direct answer may say that the earlier assessment is no longer current and that this letter replaces it for any current purpose. A short "what changed" paragraph may compare those earlier figures with the current range. If the agent did not give earlier figures, do not invent them and do not write a "what changed" section.

Voice: no em dashes, no clichés, no scarcity, no pressure, no filler. Canadian spelling (neighbourhood, honour, colour). Do not restate matrimonial, estate, or court details beyond what the client themselves raised. The letter carries the formal opinion. The email carries the plain-language breakdown.

### Before you finish

Show the agent the PDF, the Word copy, and the email draft. Remind them to check every name, address, and dollar figure against their own comps, then send it themselves.
