The short version

The breakdown

A large waterfront tract on the edge of the village, with the price now about $1 million below where it started and 559 days on the market. That points to a seller who may negotiate.

The catch is what you can build and when. Most of the land appears to carry Holding (H) zoning, there are no services to the lot, and Victoria Harbour's sewage plant is at capacity, so Tay is not allowing new homes on municipal sewer in the village until the plant is expanded. Details and sources are below.

MLS® description

What the listing says

The listing pitches 72.62 acres of private, natural vacant land with a long stretch of Sturgeon Bay shoreline, a gateway to Georgian Bay. It suggests the land suits either development or a private estate retreat, and points to the location near Midland and Waubaushene with easy access to Highway 400, under two hours from the GTA.

Location

Where it is

On the east side of Victoria Harbour, between Park Street and Sturgeon Bay. From Highway 12, turn north onto Park Street; the property is on the right, just past the Trans Canada Trail. The village shops, the Oakwood Community Centre and the Tay Township office are all close by on Park Street.

Midland, Waubaushene and Highway 400 are all minutes away.

The Victoria Harbour wastewater treatment plant sits on a separate lot along the parcel's south edge, off the end of Industrial Road. The listing's own aerial map labels it.

Due diligence

What to check before an offer

1. Zoning. The listing does not state zoning. Tay's zoning map for this area (Schedule K to Zoning By-law 2000-57, consolidated to February 2002) shows the Lot 14, Concession 8 lands east of Park Street split across several zones, most with a Holding (H) symbol: Prestige Industrial M2(H), Open Space OS(H), Village Residential R2(H) and Development D-1(H), with an Environmental Protection (EP) block toward the bay. I lined up the parcel outline from the County of Simcoe's public parcel map against the zoning map by eye, so treat this as a likely read, not a confirmation. Later amendments are filed as scanned map sheets, and I could not confirm whether any of them changed this parcel. Under section 4.7 of the by-law, land with an (H) can only keep its existing use until the Holding is lifted, which usually requires connection to full municipal services. Get a zoning confirmation from Tay's planning department.

2. Official Plan. Tay's Official Plan Schedule D (Victoria Harbour Settlement Area) puts the land east of Park Street inside the settlement boundary and shows it as Light Industrial/Commercial and Open Space, with an Environmental Protection 3 area. The Official Plan dates from 1998 and Tay has set aside money for a review, so policy could change. Homes on the part designated Light Industrial/Commercial would likely need an Official Plan amendment and a rezoning. Confirm with Tay before relying on any of this.

3. Shoreline, EP and wetland. Part of the shoreline and low ground is likely environmental protection or wetland, which would cut the buildable area. The zoning map shows EP toward the bay, and the listing gives shoreline type and frontage length as unknown. Ask Tay's planning department to confirm how much of the land is EP.

4. Servicing. Nothing is on the lot today: no hydro, water, sewer, gas, phone or cable. For sewer, Tay says the Victoria Harbour plant is operating at maximum capacity and the Township is not permitted to allow any new or additional dwelling units in Victoria Harbour until more capacity comes online. That applies to properties connected to, or required to connect to, municipal sewer. A Phase 2 expansion is funded ($29.5 million from the Province, January 2026) and being redesigned for re-tender. Tay's 2025 plant report said construction was expected to start in 2027. No completion date is set. Ask Tay whether this parcel would need to connect, and what buffer applies next to the plant.

5. Easements on title. The legal description says the land is subject to two registered instruments (RO731224 and RO1455613) and an easement over Part 1 on Plan 51R-30225 (RO1461771). The listing marks easements as unknown. Have a lawyer pull these before you firm up.

6. Survey. The listing says survey unknown and gives no lot dimensions. The parcel is irregular, with many boundary segments. Get a survey, or at least the reference plans, to confirm the frontage on the water and on Park Street.

7. Price history. One MLS® screen showed the previous price as $4,499,999, while the listing history and public sites show $3,599,000 before today's cut. The history table above follows the listing record. Worth confirming with the listing brokerage.
