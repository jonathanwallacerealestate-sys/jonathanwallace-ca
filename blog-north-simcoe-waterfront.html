How to read the 2025 and 2026 North Simcoe waterfront market

By Jonathan Wallace, REALTOR®, Faris Team Real Estate, Brokerage · Updated November 2026

Review draft. This page is not in the menu or the sitemap. Counts and listing records stay pending until the release audit. Hiding the page from search is not what keeps those records off it. They are not in the public file.

This is a reading guide for non-island waterfront in Tiny, Penetanguishene, Midland and Tay, from January 1, 2025 through November 7, 2026. The price window is $670,000 to $3,000,000. The property archive under the guide is built. It does not list homes yet.

A waterfront flag on a listing is a wide net. It can mean shoreline the owner holds directly, waterfront that is shared, or access that is indirect, such as a deeded path or a dock that is not the home's own shore. Those three stay separate here. Direct is the word on the listing. It is not a title opinion that the owner holds the shore alone.

The search asked for homes that are not on an island. That request is not the same as a title check on every row, so island status stays unverified until each row is confirmed. Homes coded as island are outside this set.

2026 on this page is year to date, January 1 through November 7. The fair comparison with 2025 uses that same run of days, not all of 2025 against part of 2026. A second control shows the full 2025 year beside 2026 year to date, and it is labeled as an unmatched pair so the two windows are not mixed by accident.

Every sale date in the saved 2025 file falls on or before November 7. That is a property of the file, not proof that November and December had no sales. A separate live query for the matched window, and for the rest of 2025, is still being checked.

The counts, means and medians below say pending review. Pending is not zero, and it is not a finished market report. Arithmetic on a saved candidate file can be reproduced. That is not a validated census of the market. Until the release audit is done, this page does not publish a sale count or a percent change.

How to read one year against the other

When the figures are released, read them in this order. Start with how many recorded sales are in the window. Then look at the mean and the median. Then look at how many of those sales sit in each price band, and what share of the sales that is. A change in the average without the band counts is an unfinished sentence.

Sold bands use the recorded sold price. Expired and terminated rows use the final asking price, and the card says so. Those asking prices do not go into the sold average.

Days on market, once they are validated, are the days on that one listing. Official cumulative days on market are a different field. A derived total across linked listings is shown only when the history is validated. An empty history is not zero days. Unknown stays unknown.

Browse listing rows

Filters use the same date window as the comparison above. They do not create listings. Until the release file is cleared, every filter ends in the same pending state.

How rows are counted

Listing rows are individual listing episodes. An expired or terminated episode stays in this list and is not labeled as a home that never sold.

Showing nothing yet. Pending review. This is not a count of sales.

No listings are available to show. Pending review is not the same as zero sales, and it is not an empty market.

A lower average is not a loss in value

Think of a classroom average. If more shorter children join the class, the average height falls. That does not mean each child got shorter. A sold-price average works the same way. More sales in a lower price band can pull the mean and the median down even when a similar home did not sell for less.

The band counts sit beside the average so that mix is visible. They still cannot say which effect happened. This page does not blame rates, demand or negotiation. Those causes are not measured here.

A small town makes this sharper. A handful of sales can swing an average when one higher sale, or one lower sale, enters the set. Once figures are released, a count under 10 is marked as a small set. Until then, every town says pending review.

What a property card shows

Each card is one listing episode, unless you switch to linked property outcomes. A confirmed link can collapse several episodes into one outcome. A shared address that is only a candidate does not collapse. The earlier expired or terminated episode then stays visible, with a pointer to the later sale when that link is confirmed. It is not counted again as a second home that failed to sell.

A card can hold up to 10 real photos when the rights are cleared and the files are actually here. A photo count from the source file is not printed in place of pictures. Missing photos stay missing. They are not repeated, and they are not drawn to fill the frame. The photo area is 16:9. If photos exist, the count sits on the image and opens a viewer. Arrow keys move, Escape closes, and focus returns to the card.

  • Bedrooms and bathrooms, or unknown.
  • Above-ground finished square feet, separate from finished square feet below ground.
  • Garage, as yes or no. Basement finish, with partial kept separate from finished.
  • Water frontage with the raw label, the raw value and the raw unit. Feet bands are used only when the unit is verified. A conflict is not given a band. Metres are not converted. Lot width and road frontage are not water frontage.
  • Lot size in the units the source used. Year built and age range stay separate.
  • Starting price, final asking price and sold price, each with its date. A missing starting price stays unknown. It is not copied from the final ask.

The sold price is the recorded sale price. It is not proof that land title has closed. Some recorded sales close after the November 7 snapshot. The title closing date stays unknown unless the source states it.

Method, sources and limits

The set is freehold and condo, for sale, in Tiny, Penetanguishene, Midland and Tay. The price window is $670,000 to $3,000,000. Sold rows are chosen by sold date and recorded sold price. Expired rows are chosen by expiry date and final asking price. Terminated rows were pulled by a termination-date window. The listing pages did not show the termination event date, so those dates stay unknown. A later update date is not used as the termination date. An expiry on December 31 stays in 2025 even if the file was updated on January 1.

Price bands do not overlap. $670,000 to under $1,500,000, then $1,500,000 to under $2,300,000, then $2,300,000 through $3,000,000. Water frontage bands, once a feet measure is verified, are under 40 ft, 40 to under 60, 60 to under 100, 100 to under 150, 150 to under 200, and 200 ft and over. Under 40 ft and unknown or unresolved rows are kept, not dropped.

Two earlier Midland expired rows are missing from the later island-no set. The reason is not documented, so this page does not guess that the island filter explains it, and it does not silently put the rows back.

Questions

Why are the averages blank?

The saved arithmetic has not been accepted as a market census. Blank means pending review. It does not mean there were no sales.

Is a lower mean a drop in home values?

No. The mean and the median describe the homes that sold in that window. They are not a repeat-sale index, and a shift in the mix of price bands can move them without any similar home selling for less.

What is the difference between a listing row and a property outcome?

A listing row is one trip onto the market: sold, expired or terminated. A property outcome groups only those trips that are confirmed to be the same property. If one of them sold, the outcome is the sale. The expired or terminated trip is not a second unsold home.

Why is some frontage unknown?

Water frontage is the shore measure, not the lot width. If the source is in metres, this page does not convert it. If two units conflict, the row is unresolved and gets no band.
