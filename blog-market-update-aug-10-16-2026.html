177 Homes Sold This Week, Up 30 Percent. The Average Price Fell $80,000 Because of It.

Market update·Week of August 10 to August 16, 2026·~2 min read

By Jonathan Wallace, REALTOR® · Week of August 10 to August 16, 2026

Simcoe County recorded 177 sales in the week of August 10 to August 16, 2026, at an average sale price of $685,032, with 8.2 months of inventory. It remains a buyer's market. The number that genuinely moved was the sales to new listing ratio, up 7 points to 34 percent.

Key takeaways

  • Months of inventory eased to 8.2 from 8.3, the first decline since the week of July 20, and still well above the 6 months that marks a buyer's market.
  • Days on market held at 49 for a second consecutive week, unchanged from the week before and up from 40 days at the start of July 2026.
  • The list to sale price ratio rose 1 point to 96 percent, so sellers collected a higher share of asking in the same week the average sale price fell.
  • 518 new listings arrived and 177 sold, yet active listings fell by 25 to 5,430. Roughly 366 listings left the board without selling, more than two for every one that sold.
  • The 10.4 percent drop in the average sale price to $685,032 reflects 30 percent more sales landing in the middle of the market, not a 10 percent fall in what homes are worth.

The headline number fell roughly $80,000 in seven days. The average sale price in Simcoe County dropped 10.4 percent, from $765,460 to $685,032. Read alone, that is the kind of move that looks like a market breaking.

The real read

It is close to the opposite. Sales rose 30.1 percent to 177, and the sellers of those 177 homes collected 96 percent of asking, a full point more than the week before. Values do not collapse in the same week that negotiating outcomes improve. Those two numbers only move in opposite directions when the composition of what sold changes.

Last week the average rose 8.5 percent because a small number of high end sales landed inside a sample of 129. This week the volume came back in the middle of the market and the average returned to where it actually lives. Two consecutive weeks where the average sale price told a story the rest of the data did not support, pointing opposite directions each time. The lesson is the same both weeks: at a sample of 130 to 180 sales, the average sale price is a measure of what sold, not of what things are worth.

Every number that describes what a seller actually experiences held or improved. The list to sale price ratio went up. Days on market held at 49. Months of inventory came down for the first time since the week of July 20. Nobody's home in Midland or Penetanguishene lost 10 percent of its value between Monday and Sunday.

The supply story

518 new listings came to market and 177 sold, yet active inventory fell by 25 to 5,430. The arithmetic leaves roughly 366 listings that left the board without a sale, more than two for every one that sold. That is the second consecutive week at close to that ratio.

It matters because it explains why the inventory picture improved without much genuine change underneath. Active listings fell while 518 new ones arrived, which means the board is clearing through withdrawals as much as through sales. The sales to new listing ratio is the cleaner measure of absorption, and at 34 percent it is a real improvement on the 26 to 29 percent range this market has been running at since the third week of July. It is also still barely a third.

The bigger picture

The national numbers landed two days after this week closed and they describe the same shape. The Canadian Real Estate Association reported on August 18 that national home sales rose 0.5 percent in July, a fourth consecutive monthly increase, while the MLS Home Price Index sat 3.3 percent below where it was a year earlier. Shaun Cathcart, CREA's Senior Economist, described July's data as a carbon copy of the June numbers. More sales at flatter prices is the national pattern, and it is exactly the shape of this week locally.

The comparison worth carrying home is supply. Canada finished July with 4.7 months of inventory. Simcoe County is running at 8.2. The national conversation about a return to balance is describing a market arriving somewhere Simcoe County went past in the spring. The gap is the whole reason a national headline about recovering sales should not change how a home in Tiny Township or Tay Township gets priced.

One more figure. The national average sale price in July was $674,819. Simcoe County's average this week was $685,032, about $10,000 above it. A county that carries Georgian Bay waterfront and cottage country sold, for one week, at close to the Canadian average. That is what the middle of the market looks like when the top of it sits out.

A buyer's market does not mean sellers lose

96 percent of asking on 30 percent more sales is a better week for sellers than the one before it, whatever the average says. The homes that sold this week sold because they were priced against the last thirty days and prepared before they went live. The roughly 366 that left the board without selling are the second half of the same sentence.

What 8.2 months and 49 days really describe is a market that will transact at a fair number and will not transact at an optimistic one. The penalty for testing a price is no longer a slightly lower sale. It is no sale, followed by a withdrawal.

What this means for you

Buying: 8.2 months of inventory still gives you selection and room to negotiate, and that has not changed. What did change is the pace. Absorption improved 7 points in a single week, so the unhurried market you have been shopping in is the slowest it has been.

Selling: plan for 49 days and budget for 96 percent of asking. Price at the front of your comparable range rather than above it. The listings leaving the board unsold are the ones that started high, and there were roughly 366 of them this week.

If you are weighing a move around Midland, Penetanguishene, Tiny, Tay or Wasaga Beach before the fall, this is a workable market. It simply pays for accuracy.
