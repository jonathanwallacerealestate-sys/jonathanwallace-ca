My house sold. What do I need to do before closing day?

Jonathan Wallace, REALTOR® · 1:06 video

The short answer

The deposit is already held safely, so the next step is a lawyer, who pays off your mortgage on closing day; bring photo ID to the first meeting. Anything listed on the first two pages of your offer stays with the house. If you're on septic and the offer asks for a pump-out and inspection, get it done before the snow, keep the driveway clear, and move your hydro, gas, internet and phone, but keep your home insurance until the lawyer says it has closed. Closing can happen any time between 10 a.m. and 5 p.m., so plan movers around a late closing, be ready to leave by noon, and leave every key and garage door remote inside.

Your checklist from offer to keys

  1. Deposit: It's already held safely in trust, so nothing for you to do here.
  2. Lawyer: Line one up early. They'll pay off your mortgage on closing day and work out the details with the buyer's lawyer. Bring valid photo ID to your first meeting and answer their questions.
  3. What's included: Read the first two pages of your offer. Anything listed there stays with the house.
  4. Septic: If your offer calls for a pump-out and inspection, book it before the ground freezes. Winter pump-outs cost more, and dig up the lids ahead of time if you don't have risers.
  5. Buyer visits: Usually two, about an hour each, with their agent. They'll measure, bring family, maybe a contractor. It's information only, it doesn't change the deal.
  6. Upkeep: Keep the grass cut. In winter, keep the driveway clear, including that plow snowbank at the end.
  7. Services: Cancel or transfer hydro, gas, internet and phone.
  8. Insurance: Keep it until your lawyer confirms the sale has closed.
  9. Closing time: It can happen anytime from 10 to 5, once the money's in and the house is in the buyer's name. Be ready to leave by noon on closing day.
  10. Keys: Leave them all inside. House, shed, mailbox, garage, and the garage door remotes.

This is general information, and your lawyer will confirm what applies to your sale.
