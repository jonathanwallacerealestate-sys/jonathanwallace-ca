January to May 2026: A Cold Start, Then Simcoe County Caught Up to 2025

Market review·January to May 2026 in review·~7 min read
Thinking of selling? Book a home evaluation

By Jonathan Wallace, REALTOR® · January to May 2026 in review

Simcoe County recorded 2,939 sales from January to May 2026, just 14 fewer than the 2,953 of the same five months in 2025. The total hides two very different halves. January and February ran well behind 2025. From March on, every month sold more homes than a year earlier, and by May months of inventory had fallen to 6.25 from 9.05 in January.

Key takeaways

  • January 2026 sold 372 homes, 27.3 percent fewer than January 2025, with 9.05 months of inventory.
  • March was the first month to beat 2025 on sales. April was up 12.0 percent and May up 10.2 percent.
  • Months of inventory fell every month, from 9.05 in January to 6.25 in May.
  • The average sale price was below 2025 in all five months, but the gap narrowed from about 12 percent in January to 4.1 percent in May.
  • Severn and Wasaga Beach finished May at their tightest levels of the spring. Midland and Penetanguishene was the only Georgian Bay market to sell fewer homes than in early 2025.

Why this review covers five months

These are the first five months of 2026, looked at together, using the board figures as revised in November 2026. Reading them side by side shows something a single month cannot: how the spring market built, and where it got to before summer.

Winter: a slower start than 2025

January 2026 sold 372 homes, down from 512 a year earlier. Homes took 58 days to sell, and sold at 96.2 percent of asking. With 3,365 homes for sale and so few buyers, months of inventory was 9.05, deep into buyer's market territory.

February improved to 458 sales but was still 8.9 percent behind February 2025. Buyers who were out in the snow had the market to themselves.

March to May: the catch-up

March sold 547 homes, 16 more than March 2025. April sold 719, up 12.0 percent. May sold 843, up 10.2 percent. Sellers were more cautious than in 2025. New listings for the five months were 10,341, compared with 10,926, a drop of 5.4 percent. More sales from fewer new listings is why months of inventory kept falling.

By May the county sat at 6.25 months of inventory, still a buyer's market, but the closest to balanced it had been all year. May 2025 had been at 6.98.

Prices lagged the recovery

The average sale price sat below 2025 in every one of the five months. In January and March it was about 12 percent lower. By May the gap was 4.1 percent, at $777,180 against $810,707. Homes also sold a little further from asking than in 2025, at 97.0 percent in May compared with 97.5. Buyers came back in numbers before they came back on price.

Midland and Penetanguishene

The one Bay market that did not catch up. 130 sales against 142, down 8.5 percent. May was the clearest gap: 31 sales against 42 a year earlier. Active listings climbed from 164 in January to 274 in May, and months of inventory finished May at 8.84. Homes that did sell in May went at 97.7 percent of asking in 39 days, so well-priced homes were not the problem. Supply was.

Tay Township and Tiny Township

Level with 2025 at 144 sales against 143. The area started the year at 16.76 months of inventory in January, the deepest of the six markets, and came down to 11.48 by May. It remained one of the most supplied markets on the Bay through the spring.

Wasaga Beach

The biggest turnaround. In February there were 15.00 months of inventory and only 20 sales. By May, 69 homes sold and months of inventory was down to 6.96, below May 2025. Over the five months Wasaga sold 213 homes, eight more than in 2025.

Severn

Up 9.2 percent on sales, the strongest gain of the six. May alone had 31 sales against 21 a year earlier, which pushed months of inventory down to 5.94, under the six-month buyer's market line for the first time in 2026.

Collingwood and The Blue Mountains

311 sales against 304, but supply grew faster. Months of inventory finished May at 9.61, up from 8.07 a year earlier. This market spans Simcoe County and Grey County, so it is not counted inside the county totals.

Orillia

Steady at 178 sales against 177. Orillia stayed among the tightest markets in the region, at 5.47 months of inventory in May, though that was looser than its 4.18 in May 2025.

What the first five months told us

Buying: winter was the window. January and February had the fewest competing buyers of the year and the longest selling times. If you are watching for next year, the same pattern is worth planning around.

Selling: spring buyers showed up in 2026, but they did not pay 2025 prices. Average prices ran 4 to 12 percent below the same month of 2025, so a list price built on last year's numbers was likely too high.

Common questions about this market

How many homes sold in Simcoe County from January to May 2026?

2,939, adding the five monthly board counts, compared with 2,953 in the same months of 2025.

When did the Simcoe County market pick up in 2026?

March. It was the first month of 2026 to sell more homes than the same month of 2025, at 547 against 531. April and May were each more than 10 percent ahead.

Was Simcoe County a buyer's market in spring 2026?

Yes. Months of inventory fell from 9.05 in January to 6.25 in May but stayed above six months, the line for a buyer's market, in every month.

Which Georgian Bay market was strongest in spring 2026?

Severn had the biggest sales gain, up 9.2 percent, and finished May at 5.94 months of inventory. Wasaga Beach had the biggest turnaround, from 15.00 months in February to 6.96 in May.

How long did homes take to sell in Simcoe County in early 2026?

58 days in January, falling to 40 days by May. When a listing is cancelled and put back on the market, the days on market count starts over, so some homes have been for sale longer than the number shows.
