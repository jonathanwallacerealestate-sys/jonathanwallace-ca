What does the Bank of Canada rate hike mean for buyers and sellers on Georgian Bay?

Jonathan Wallace, REALTOR® · 1:03 video

The short answer

The quarter point increase, from 2.25% to 2.5%, is less about the size of the move and more about the message: the Bank of Canada is serious about inflation. Buyers holding pre-approvals may rush to use them first, then some will cool off as budgets shrink. For prices in Midland, Penetanguishene, Tiny, Tay and Wasaga Beach, nobody knows yet, but if buying gets harder to afford, prices could soften a little more.

What Jonathan Wallace said

Well, that's not the news anybody wanted. The rates, they just went up. The Bank of Canada just raised its key rate today for the first time since July of 2023. Now, it's only a quarter point, from two and a quarter to two and a half. So this isn't really about the size of the move, it's about the message. The bank is telling everybody that they're serious about fighting inflation. Gas, diesel and heating oil have all been really expensive for months, and they don't want that spilling into the price of everything else.

So what does that mean for buyers? In the short term, you might actually see a quick rush. People have already locked in pre-approval rates, and they'll want to use them before they run out. And then after that, some buyers may cool off a bit. Higher rates mean a smaller budget.

And for prices, honestly, nobody knows yet. If buying gets harder to afford, we could see home prices here in Midland, Penetang, Tiny, Tay and Wasaga Beach soften a little bit more. If you've been thinking about buying or selling and you're wondering what it means for you, send me a message.
