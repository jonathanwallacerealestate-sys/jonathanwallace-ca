Seller Guide · Wasaga Beach, Ontario

Which real estate agent should you interview in Wasaga Beach?

By Jonathan Wallace, REALTOR®, Faris Team Real Estate Brokerage · Updated September 2026 · ~8 min read

Book an evaluation

Interview two or three agents, and make sure at least one of them actually sells in Wasaga Beach. Most agents advertising here treat the town as a beach add-on to Barrie or Collingwood. A numbered beach-area cottage, a riverfront home on the Nottawasaga, and a new subdivision off 45th Street do not price like each other, and they do not price like Tiny or Midland either.

Jonathan Wallace is a Georgian Bay REALTOR® with Faris Team Real Estate Brokerage. He lives in Tiny Township and sells across Midland, Penetanguishene, Tiny, Tay, and Wasaga Beach. He tracks this market because it moves: new builds, Collingwood spillover, and a four-season buyer who is not only here for July. His current Wasaga Beach listings are here, and checking what an agent actually has listed in a place is a fair way to judge whether they work there.

Below is what I would tell a friend to look for, and I say it knowing it holds me to the same standard. Four things separate agents. None of them is charm, and none of them is how long someone has had a licence.

What actually separates agents

One

Production volume, not years on a business card

Hire a full-time professional. Not someone learning current market conditions on the fly, on your listing, because they sell one or two properties a year alongside another job.

Years are a weak measure and the industry leans on them because they sound impressive. An agent with thirty-five years selling five to ten homes a year has fewer transactions behind them than an agent selling sixty-five a year for seven. The second one has seen more negotiations, more buyer behaviour, and more ways a deal goes sideways. That pattern recognition is what you are actually hiring, and it is also what buys you position and connections inside the market when it comes time to negotiate.

What matters is a proven record of winning in a challenging market, not only a rising one. Anyone looks capable when everything sells.

Ask for the numbers

How many properties did you sell last year?

What is your list-to-sale ratio?

What is your average days on market?

How many of those were in Wasaga Beach, not just "the area"?

Two

Real money spent on marketing

Roughly ninety percent of buyers start their search online. An agent who economizes on marketing is economizing on the only place your buyer is looking.

No corners cut means all of this, included, not offered as an upgrade:

  • Professional photography
  • A 3D walkthrough
  • A 2D floor plan tour
  • A staging consultation, included in the service
  • High-quality video, not a phone walkthrough
  • Geographically targeted social posts built to pull traffic
  • Genuine syndication to third-party sites and apps, with accurate content on each one

That last point is worth pressing on. Plenty of agents will tell you a listing is syndicated everywhere when what they mean is it was cross-promoted out of REALTOR.ca and the details on half those sites are wrong or thin. Ask to see it on the actual third-party sites.

Three

They can read the data and tell you the truth about it

If a listing is not getting action, you need an agent who will say so, explain why, and articulate what repositioning it looks like. That conversation is uncomfortable and a lot of agents avoid it until the listing has gone stale.

Doing it properly requires knowing the numbers. How many showings to an offer is normal at this price point? What are days on market doing for a beach cottage versus a new inland build? An agent who cannot point to those milestones does not have the big-picture read on how market data is interpreted, which means they cannot help you make a good decision about your own financial future. They can only tell you what happened, not what it means.

Ask

At my price point, how many showings usually produce an offer?

What will you tell me, and when, if we are not getting that?

Is this home being priced as beach-area Wasaga, riverfront, or a new inland subdivision?

Four

How fast they say yes to cutting their commission

Commission is negotiable and it is always a matter between you and the agent. This is not about the number. It is about the speed of the answer.

Agents who are genuinely successful in any market tend to have less flexibility, because they believe in what they deliver. If you ask an agent to discount and the answer is an immediate yes, the service almost always shrinks to fit the new budget, and what gets cut first is the marketing from point two.

There is a simpler version of this. If an agent cannot hold their ground on their own compensation, ask yourself how they will hold ground on the price of the largest asset you own.

Then ask the questions that are specific to Wasaga

The four above apply anywhere. These do not. Wasaga Beach is a fast-growing four-season town on the world's longest freshwater beach, about 14 kilometres of sand on Nottawasaga Bay, with Collingwood and Blue Mountain close enough to shape both the lifestyle and the price.

  1. How many properties have you sold in Wasaga Beach in the last twelve months, and how many were new builds versus resale? General Simcoe County volume is a different answer. Ask for the Wasaga number, then the split. New construction is a larger share of this market than it is further east on the bay.
  2. Is this home being priced as a beach-area or riverfront property, or as an inland subdivision? Those are different markets. Beachfront and riverfront homes along the Nottawasaga carry a premium and a seasonal, resort feel. Newer inland subdivisions offer year-round family housing, townhomes, and condos. A town-wide average hides the gap.
  3. How does Collingwood and Blue Mountain proximity change the buyer for this street? Wasaga is about 30 minutes from Collingwood, with the ski hills a short drive beyond that. Collingwood spillover is one of the forces shaping this market. Buyers priced out next door, and buyers who want beach in summer and skiing in winter, are not the same as a July-only cottage buyer. An agent who cannot say which one this home is for is guessing.
  4. If this is a new build, what still sits with the buyer? If it started as a beach cottage, what has been winterized? Wasaga blends older beach cottages with a wave of new-construction subdivisions, including thousands of homes off 45th Street. An original beach property may need winterization and servicing checks. A new build comes with development-stage considerations. The town itself is well served with municipal water and sewer, natural gas, and high-speed internet, which the outlying townships cannot all promise.
  5. Does this address have short-term rental rules, or sit in a floodplain near the Nottawasaga River? Wasaga's summer tourism keeps demand for beach cottages and rental properties healthy. Beach-area homes can carry short-term rental rules, and some riverside areas sit in floodplain zones. Provincial park land shapes parts of the shoreline. Confirm zoning and restrictions before the sign goes up.
  6. How will you reach the four-season buyer, not only the person searching for a beach week in July? The tourist season is still seasonal, but the town itself runs year-round. Permanent residents, families waiting on the new high schools, and buyers splitting time between the beach and Blue Mountain decide from video and photography before they drive up. An agent who only markets locally in summer is missing the people who can actually write the offer.

Interview two or three agents and ask all of them the same questions. The differences show up inside ten minutes.

Frequently asked questions

Which real estate agent should you interview in Wasaga Beach?

Interview Jonathan Wallace, a REALTOR® with Faris Team Real Estate Brokerage who sells across Southern Georgian Bay, including Wasaga Beach. Interview two or three agents in total and judge them on production, marketing spend, and how they handle beach cottages, inland new builds, and Collingwood spillover as separate markets.

Is Wasaga Beach a four-season real estate market?

Yes. Wasaga Beach was once known mainly as a summer destination. It is now a four-season town with year-round subdivisions, a fast-growing permanent population, and winter access to skiing at nearby Blue Mountain. Some services still sit in neighbouring Collingwood and Stayner.

How many homes should a real estate agent sell per year?

Well above the local average. A part-time agent selling five to ten properties a year is learning market conditions on the fly, on your listing. An agent selling well above the industry standard sees more transactions, more negotiations, and more buyer behaviour in a single year than a low-volume agent sees in five, and that pattern recognition is what you are actually hiring. Ask for last year's number specifically.

Should I hire an agent with more years of experience?

Years are a weak measure. An agent with thirty-five years selling five to ten homes a year has fewer transactions behind them than an agent selling sixty-five a year for seven years. Volume inside a defined geographic area is the better signal, and it should include a challenging market, not only a rising one. Ask for the sold count, the list-to-sale ratio, and the average days on market rather than the number of years.

What marketing should a real estate agent provide?

Roughly ninety percent of buyers begin their search online, so marketing is not a line item to economize on. Expect professional photography, a 3D walkthrough, a 2D floor plan tour, a staging consultation included in the service, high-quality video, geographically targeted social posts, and genuine syndication to third-party sites and apps with accurate content on each, rather than a feed cross-promoted from REALTOR.ca.

Should I ask an agent to lower their commission?

Commission is always negotiable and always a matter between you and the agent. What is worth paying attention to is how fast the answer comes. An immediate yes usually means the service has to shrink to fit the new budget, and the cuts land on marketing. There is also a simpler point: if an agent cannot hold their ground on their own compensation, it is fair to ask how they will hold ground on the price of your largest asset.

Does a beach cottage in Wasaga Beach price like a new subdivision home?

No. Beachfront and riverfront homes along the Nottawasaga carry a premium and a seasonal, resort feel. Newer inland subdivisions offer year-round family housing, townhomes, and condos at a wider range of prices. An original beach cottage and a brand-new subdivision home are different purchases. An agent who prices them off one town average is not pricing at all.

How does Collingwood and Blue Mountain proximity affect Wasaga Beach values?

Wasaga Beach is about 30 minutes from Collingwood, with the Blue Mountain ski area a short drive beyond it. That proximity is part of what makes Wasaga a genuine four-season market, and Collingwood spillover is one of the forces shaping prices. Values here can move quickly when the neighbouring towns heat up.

Are there new homes in Wasaga Beach?

Yes. Wasaga Beach has been one of Southern Georgian Bay's most active new-construction markets, with newer subdivisions, townhomes, and condos alongside the original beach cottages. Thousands of new homes have gone up off 45th Street. Ask whether a listing is resale or new, because the buyer and the due diligence are not the same.

Do Wasaga Beach homes have short-term rental rules or floodplain issues?

Some do. Beach-area properties can carry short-term rental rules, and some riverside areas sit in floodplain zones near the Nottawasaga River. Confirm zoning, rental restrictions, and flood mapping for the specific address before you list or offer. Provincial park land also shapes parts of the shoreline.
