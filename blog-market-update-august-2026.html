August Was the Fifth Straight Month Simcoe County Outsold 2025. It Was Also the Last.

Monthly market update·August 2026 in review·~6 min read
Thinking of selling? Book a home evaluation

By Jonathan Wallace, REALTOR® · August 2026 in review

Simcoe County recorded 706 sales in August 2026 at an average sale price of $724,971, with 7.60 months of inventory. That is still a buyer's market, but a slightly less crowded one than a year earlier. August 2025 had 694 sales and 7.88 months of inventory. From April through August, every month of 2026 sold more homes than the same month of 2025. September broke the run.

Key takeaways

  • 706 sales, up 1.7 percent on August 2025, from 10.1 percent fewer new listings.
  • Months of inventory was 7.60, below the 7.88 of August 2025.
  • The average sale price was $724,971, down 4.9 percent from a year earlier.
  • Midland and Penetanguishene sold 46 homes, its busiest month of 2026, and was the closest Bay market to balanced outside Orillia.
  • Tay and Tiny sold the same 43 homes as last August, and homes sold 23 days faster.

Five months in a row, then a stop

It is easy to read 2026 as one long slide. The sales line says otherwise. April, May, June, July and August each beat the same month of 2025. In every one of those months, months of inventory also sat below the year before. Buyers were not rushing, but there were more of them than in 2025.

August was the smallest of those five wins, at 12 more sales than a year earlier. In September the county sold 658 homes against 796 a year earlier, and the run was over. Looking back, August is where the summer momentum ran out.

Fewer sellers did the heavy lifting

The better inventory number did not come from a wave of buyers. It came from sellers holding back. 2,032 new listings came on in August, 229 fewer than August 2025. Active listings finished at 5,364, down 1.9 percent from a year earlier.

Prices did not get the same lift. The average sale price was 4.9 percent below last August and homes sold at 96.7 percent of asking, exactly where they were a year earlier. Days on market rose to 45 from 43. More homes sold, at lower prices, and buyers still had time to decide.

Midland and Penetanguishene

The standout. 46 sales was the highest monthly count of 2026 and up from 33 last August. New listings fell to 93 from 119, so months of inventory dropped to 6.15, the tightest Bay market outside Orillia and close to the six-month line. The lower average price, $531,703, suggests more of the activity was at the affordable end of town.

Tay Township and Tiny Township

Same number of sales as last August, 43, and about the same months of inventory at 11.91. The difference was speed. Homes sold in 48 days compared with 71 a year earlier, and at 95.0 percent of asking compared with 94.4. With 43 sales, a handful of higher-priced homes can lift the average that much, so the 15.2 percent jump is not a sign that values rose across the area.

Wasaga Beach

A softer August than last year. Sales slipped to 51 and days on market rose to 55 from 42, second slowest of the six markets behind Collingwood. Homes sold at 96.1 percent of asking. With 501 homes for sale, buyers had choice and used it.

Severn

Flat on sales at 26, but the quickest market of the six. Homes sold in 33 days, compared with 55 a year earlier. Months of inventory was higher at 8.58, because more homes were listed: 223 active compared with 193.

Collingwood and The Blue Mountains

The weakest month of the six. Sales fell 16.9 percent to 59 and months of inventory rose to 11.93. Homes sold at 96.7 percent of asking, better than last year, so the buyers who did act were paying closer to list. This market spans Simcoe County and Grey County, so it is not counted inside the county totals.

Orillia

The only market under six months of inventory, at 4.94, and up 17.4 percent on sales. Days on market did lengthen to 53 from 40, so even here buyers took their time.

What this means for you

Buying: the most active market on our side of the Bay in August was Midland and Penetanguishene, at an average under $532,000. If that is your price range, expect competition from other buyers on the best homes. In Tay, Tiny and Wasaga Beach, nine to twelve months of supply still gives you room on price and terms.

Selling: a market where more homes sell than last year can still be a buyer's market. August proved buyers were there, at the right price. Look at what sold in your community in the last 60 to 90 days and price to that, not to the listings still sitting.

Common questions about this market

How many homes sold in Simcoe County in August 2026?

706, up 1.7 percent from 694 in August 2025, at an average sale price of $724,971.

Was August 2026 a buyer's market in Simcoe County?

Yes. Months of inventory was 7.60. Anything above six months is a buyer's market. It was a little less of one than August 2025, which sat at 7.88.

What happened in Midland and Penetanguishene in August 2026?

46 homes sold, the most of any month in 2026, up from 33 a year earlier. Months of inventory fell to 6.15 and the average sale price was $531,703.

How fast were homes selling in Tay Township and Tiny Township in August 2026?

Homes that sold averaged 48 days on market, down from 71 in August 2025. When a listing is cancelled and put back on the market, the days on market count starts over, so some homes have been for sale longer than the number shows.

Did Simcoe County sell more homes in 2026 than in 2025 over the summer?

Yes. April through August 2026 each had more sales than the same month in 2025. September 2026 was the first month after that run to fall short, at 658 sales against 796.
