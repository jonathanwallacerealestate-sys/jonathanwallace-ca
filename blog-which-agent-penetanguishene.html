Which real estate agent should you interview in Penetanguishene?

By Jonathan Wallace, REALTOR, Faris Team Real Estate Brokerage · Updated August 2026 · ~8 min read

Interview two or three agents, and make sure at least one of them actually sells in Penetanguishene. Most agents advertising here run the town as an add-on to Midland or Barrie. The waterfront pockets, the bilingual buyer, and the school and tax questions are not the same as Midland's, even though the two towns sit one valley apart.

Below is what I would tell a friend to look for, and I say it knowing it holds me to the same standard. Four things separate agents. None of them is charm, and none of them is how long someone has had a licence.

What actually separates agents

One

Production volume, not years on a business card

Hire a full-time professional. Not someone learning current market conditions on the fly, on your listing, because they sell one or two properties a year alongside another job.

Years are a weak measure and the industry leans on them because they sound impressive. An agent with thirty-five years selling five to ten homes a year has fewer transactions behind them than an agent selling sixty-five a year for seven. The second one has seen more negotiations, more buyer behaviour, and more ways a deal goes sideways. That pattern recognition is what you are actually hiring.

What matters is a proven record of winning in a challenging market, not only a rising one. Anyone looks capable when everything sells.

Ask for the numbers

How many properties did you sell last year?

What is your list-to-sale ratio?

What is your average days on market?

How many of those were in Penetanguishene, not just "the area"?

Two

Real money spent on marketing

Roughly ninety percent of buyers start their search online. An agent who economizes on marketing is economizing on the only place your buyer is looking.

No corners cut means all of this, included, not offered as an upgrade:

  • Professional photography
  • A 3D walkthrough
  • A 2D floor plan tour
  • A staging consultation, included in the service
  • High-quality video, not a phone walkthrough
  • Geographically targeted social posts built to pull traffic
  • Genuine syndication to third-party sites and apps, with accurate content on each one

That last point is worth pressing on. Plenty of agents will tell you a listing is syndicated everywhere when what they mean is it was cross-promoted out of REALTOR.ca and the details on half those sites are wrong or thin. Ask to see it on the actual third-party sites.

In Penetanguishene, ask one more thing: how will you reach both English and Francophone buyers? The town is officially bilingual. An agent who only writes the listing in one language is leaving a real part of the pool out.

Three

They can read the data and tell you the truth about it

If a listing is not getting action, you need an agent who will say so, explain why, and articulate what repositioning it looks like. That conversation is uncomfortable and a lot of agents avoid it until the listing has gone stale.

Doing it properly requires knowing the numbers. How many showings to an offer is normal at this price point? What are days on market doing in this specific pocket? An agent who cannot point to those milestones cannot help you make a good decision about your own financial future.

Ask

At my price point, how many showings usually produce an offer?

What will you tell me, and when, if we are not getting that?

Is this home being priced as in-town Penetanguishene, or as waterfront in a named pocket?

Four

How fast they say yes to cutting their commission

Commission is negotiable and it is always a matter between you and the agent. This is not about the number. It is about the speed of the answer.

Agents who are genuinely successful in any market tend to have less flexibility, because they believe in what they deliver. If you ask an agent to discount and the answer is an immediate yes, the service almost always shrinks to fit the new budget, and what gets cut first is the marketing from point two.

There is a simpler version of this. If an agent cannot hold their ground on their own compensation, ask yourself how they will hold ground on the price of the largest asset you own.

Then ask the questions that are specific to Penetanguishene

The four above apply anywhere. These do not. Penetanguishene is a bilingual harbour town of about 10,000 people. It shares a valley with Midland and almost nothing about a waterfront sale here is the same as an in-town Midland resale.

  1. How many properties have you sold in Penetanguishene in the last twelve months, and how many were on water? General Simcoe County volume is a different answer. Ask for the Penetanguishene number.
  2. Which pocket is this home in, and how does pricing here differ from the one next to it? Beck Boulevard does not price like Champlain Road. Sandy Bay does not price like an in-town street near Main Street. Gordon and Gilwood are their own shoreline. A town-wide average hides all of it.
  3. On this stretch of shoreline, does the property own down to the water, or is there a shore road allowance in between? This is not visible on a listing. An agent who works this shoreline answers by address. One who does not will guess.
  4. Is this home on municipal services, or on a well and septic? If it started life as a seasonal cottage, what has been winterized, and what still needs to be? Penetanguishene has more original cottages and waterfront stock than Midland. Servicing and winterization are live conditions here, not footnotes.
  5. How will you market this home to both English and Francophone buyers? "We can translate the listing" is not a plan. Ask what actually goes out, in which language, and where.
  6. Can you explain the school picture in one minute, without making it a surprise in a showing? There is currently no English-language high school in town, so English-stream high schoolers are bused to Midland. Burkevale Protestant Separate School is a genuine draw and families drive in for it. The right agent puts that on the table early.
  7. What is the exact property tax on this home, and how will you present it? Taxes on a similar home tend to run slightly higher in Penetanguishene than in Midland. Buyers ask. An agent who waits until the offer stage to find the number is handing the other side a late objection.

Interview two or three agents and ask all of them the same questions. The differences show up inside ten minutes.

Do waterfront properties in Penetanguishene own down to the water?

It depends on the property. Some shoreline homes own to the water. Others have a shore road allowance between the house and the bay. This is one of the largest variables in what a Penetanguishene waterfront property is worth, and it is not something you can determine from a listing. Ask before the property is priced.

Are homes in Penetanguishene on wells and septic?

Many in-town homes are on municipal water and wastewater. A larger share of the original cottages and waterfront stock sit on a well and septic, or started as seasonal and were winterized later. Confirm servicing and winterization before you list or offer. Leaving that to a buyer's conditions is how deals get messy.

Should I market a Penetanguishene listing in French and English?

Where it helps, yes. Penetanguishene is officially bilingual with a strong Francophone community. The right approach depends on the property and the likely buyer. An agent who works this town should be able to say what actually goes out in each language, not just that a translation is possible.

Where do Penetanguishene students go to high school in English?

There is currently no English-language high school in Penetanguishene, so English-stream high schoolers are bused to Midland. Burkevale Protestant Separate School (JK to 8) is well regarded and draws families from outside town. Put both facts in the conversation early.

Are property taxes higher in Penetanguishene than in Midland?

On a similar home, they tend to run slightly higher, because a similar municipal load is spread across fewer residents. It is rarely a dealbreaker. Present the exact figure up front so it does not become a late objection.

An immediate yes usually means the service has to shrink to fit the new budget, and the cuts land on marketing.
